## 1. The thesis

### 1.1 The question

Given the diagnosis from Q1 through Q4 and the meta-analyses (LAFI, historical cohort, KAT retroactive), what specific archetypes of roster moves would most plausibly improve the Wolves' championship odds, and what are the realistic acquisition paths for each?

This is the punchline of the entire project. Every previous analysis has been diagnostic. Q5 is prescriptive. It's the section the front office will read first if they read anything.

### 1.2 Why this matters

A diagnostic that doesn't lead to action is academic. The Wolves front office has decisions to make: extend or trade Randle (player option), evaluate Gobert's future, decide on Naz's role, fill the DiVincenzo hole, address bench production. Q5 organizes the project's findings into evidence-based recommendations for those decisions.

But the structure matters. Q5 prescribes **archetypes**, not names. This is critical. Here's why.

Front offices don't acquire from a list of names; they acquire from a list of archetypes filtered by availability and price. "We need a secondary creator who can run pick-and-roll and shoot at 36% from three at high volume" is an actionable framing. "We should sign Tyus Jones" is a fan take. The first framing leads to a search across the league for the best fit and value. The second framing pre-commits to a specific player who may or may not be the best fit and may or may not be acquirable.

Names go in an appendix. The prescription itself is about gaps and the kinds of players who fill them.

### 1.3 The principles

**Honest about cost.** Every recommendation has a price (cap dollars, draft picks, players given up). The prescription must engage with cost, not just describe what would help.

**Honest about availability.** Some archetypes are easy to find. Some are extremely scarce. The prescription should distinguish between "go get a wing shooter" (easy) and "go get a top-15 creator under 30" (extremely hard).

**Probabilistic, not deterministic.** No move is guaranteed to work. The prescription is about which moves have the highest expected value, not which moves will definitely succeed.

**Multi-path, not single-path.** Multiple combinations of moves could plausibly work. The prescription should sketch several paths, not one.

**First principles, not pattern matching.** LAFI v1 established that the Wolves' Q4 pathology is rare and not well-precedented. Prescription leans on the diagnosed mechanism (Q1-Q4, LAFI, Q8), not on copying what worked for similar teams, because few similar teams exist. Where historical evidence exists (Q0C team-level, Q7 star-level), use it; where it does not, derive from mechanism. Forcing historical analogies that do not fit is worse than acknowledging that the analogy set is thin.

## 2. The three paths and the gap analysis

### 2.1 The three independent fixable paths (from LAFI v1)

LAFI v1's diagnosis surfaced three independent paths that, individually or in combination, would close the Wolves' Q4 architectural gap. These three paths are the spine of the Q5 prescription. Every gap analysis below is organized under one of them.

**Path 1: Edwards develops into Category A solo creation.** Edwards bends defenses one-on-one to a degree that manufactures shot quality despite motion death. Realistic over multiple seasons. Not in this immediate playoff window. Q0B's Edwards trajectory analysis and Q7's comp work feed this path.

**Path 2: Category B catch-and-shoot personnel acquisition.** Elite catch-and-shoot specialists produce good shots from stationary positions, compensating for the lack of off-ball motion. Acquirable via trade and free agency; buyer's market exists for shooters. The most acquisition-driven of the three paths.

**Path 3: System change via restored off-ball motion.** The LAFI allocation finding showed the Wolves' playbook is normal-breadth (Q4 Action Poverty at 45th percentile) but iso has displaced PR-Ball-Handler. Coaching can restore the 2023-24 allocation. Cheapest path; no roster moves required. Path 3's ceiling is bounded but real.

### 2.2 The gap inventory under the three paths

From Q1 through Q4, Q6, Q8, plus the meta-analyses, compile the specific gaps and assign each to its primary path.

**Path 1 (Edwards development) gaps:**
- Edwards has not yet reached the tier where his solo creation manufactures shot quality despite motion death (LAFI, Q0B). Time-of-possession share at 21.6% indicates defenses still scheme him with help.
- Interim: a secondary creator who can punish doubles while Edwards develops (Q3 finding: Ant gets blitzed, no one else punishes). This is Path 1 support, not Path 1 itself.

**Path 2 (catch-and-shoot personnel) gaps:**
- DiVincenzo lost for the year (Achilles); was the team's elite specialist at 38.3% on 496 catch-and-shoot 3PA (LAFI Section 6).
- No other player at Klay-Thompson-tier volume and accuracy (Naz 38.1%/344, McDaniels 45.1% but only 162 attempts, Dosunmu 42.9%/231 at lower volume).
- The Category B threshold for closing the LAFI C5 gap is approximately 85th percentile of league catch-and-shoot 3P% at 5+ attempts per game.

**Path 3 (system change) gaps:**
- Iso has risen from 7-8% to 9.6% of possessions; PR-Ball-Handler has dropped from 14-16% to 13.1% (LAFI Section 6). The actions exist; they are being called less.
- Off-ball motion (Cut + OffScreen) has been flat at 10-11% across all five Edwards-era seasons. Restoring the 2023-24 design pattern would lift motion and reallocate possessions.
- This is a coaching path, not an acquisition path. Q0D's findings inform what specifically should change.

**Defensive and structural gaps (preserved from original Q5 spec):**
- Point of attack defense, especially against quick guards (Q3c)
- Switchability with Gobert at the 5 (Q3c, Q8)
- Wing depth (Q2, including DiVincenzo's loss)
- No answer for skilled-five offenses (Q4)
- Timeline mismatch between core (24-26) and veterans (30, 33) (Q0B)

These defensive and structural gaps cross paths. Some are addressed by Path 2 (a defensive-utility catch-and-shoot wing closes both Category B and wing depth). Some are independent and require their own prescription line.

### 2.3 Prioritizing the gaps

Within each path, prioritize gaps by:

- Magnitude of impact (how much would closing this gap improve net rating? quantified from Q2 lineup substitution and LAFI component shift estimates)
- Frequency of relevance (how often does this gap matter?)
- Closability (how realistic is it to close this gap given cap and assets?)

Across paths, prioritize by:

- Expected closure timing (Path 3 can move in months; Path 2 in one offseason; Path 1 across 2 seasons)
- Cost (Path 3 is free; Path 2 is roster capital; Path 1 is patience + Edwards' development)
- Robustness (Path 3 alone is insufficient against elite-rim-protection opponents per Q4 finding; Path 2 + Path 3 is the most defensible combination if Path 1 has not yet materialized)

### 2.4 The archetype framework (preserved and remapped to paths)

The original Q5 archetype work is preserved here, remapped onto the three paths. Archetypes are defined by production thresholds, not by name.

**Path 2 archetype: high-volume catch-and-shoot wing (Category B specialist)**
- Required production: 38%+ three-point shooting on 5+ catch-and-shoot 3PA per game, ideally with switchable defense at the 3.
- Defensive minimum: capable of defending wings without being a complete liability.
- Age and contract: realistic acquisition target (under 32, on a movable contract).
- Maps to the "shooting" piece of the original Q5 archetype work.

**Path 1 interim support archetype: secondary creator**
- Required production: pick-and-roll PPP > 0.95 vs blitz, three-point shooting at 35%+ on 5+ attempts per game, on-ball usage rate 18-24%.
- Defensive minimum: not a sieve; capable of defending a position.
- Age and contract: realistic acquisition target (under 32, on a movable contract).
- Note: this archetype is interim. If Path 1 succeeds (Edwards reaches Luka/SGA tier), the secondary creator becomes less critical. Acquire only if available at the right cost; do not overpay.

**Path 3 archetype: stretch big with defensive utility (relevant if Gobert is moved per Q8)**
- Required production: 36%+ three-point shooting on 4+ attempts per game, defensive rebound rate > 18%, switchable on perimeter for short bursts.
- Defensive minimum: positive defensive impact metrics.
- Note: this archetype is conditional on the Q8 verdict on Gobert. If Gobert stays, this is a complement; if Gobert moves, this is a replacement.

**Cross-path archetype: defensive utility wing**
- Closes the wing-depth gap (Q2) and the point-of-attack defense gap (Q3c).
- Production thresholds: positive defensive impact metrics, league-average shooting at minimum, position defensible.
- Cross-references Path 2 (if the wing can also catch-and-shoot at Category B level, the single move closes multiple gaps).

The archetype framework is the same craft as the original Q5 spec. The remap means each archetype now points back to which LAFI-derived path it serves, so the prescription's coherence with the diagnosis is visible.

## 4. The acquisition paths

For each archetype gap, identify realistic acquisition paths.

### 4.1 The path categories

**Free agency:** Sign a player whose contract is expiring. Easiest but constrained by cap space and player choice.

**Trade for star:** Trade significant assets for a high-impact player. Hardest, highest variance, most discussed.

**Trade for role player:** Trade modest assets for a fit-specific role player. The most common type of move that actually shifts a team.

**Internal development:** Bet on a current player improving. Cheapest, slowest. Path 1 (Edwards development) lives primarily here.

**Draft:** Late-first-round draft pick or development of recent picks. Very slow.

**System change (non-acquisition).** Restoring designed off-ball motion and rebalancing iso back toward pick-and-roll. Path 3 lives entirely here. The first non-acquisition path in the analysis. Required because the LAFI allocation finding showed this is the only fixable path that does not require new personnel. Q0D's analysis quantifies the realistic ceiling of system-only improvements.

### 4.2 The path for each archetype

For each prioritized archetype need, sketch the most realistic paths.

**Example for "Secondary creator":**

Free agency path: Who's a free agent next summer who fits the archetype? Tier them by realism (Tier 1: realistic targets, Tier 2: stretch targets, Tier 3: unrealistic but listed for completeness).

Trade path: What players around the league fit the archetype and could plausibly be acquired? What trade structures would work?

Internal path: Could Shannon Jr. develop into the archetype? Could Edwards' playmaking improve enough to compensate?

### 4.3 The path comparison

For each path, evaluate:
- Probability of success (can we actually get this done?)
- Cost (what do we give up?)
- Time horizon (when does this help?)
- Risk (what's the downside?)

Some paths dominate others. A free agency signing of a fit-specific role player is usually better than a star trade for fit reasons because it preserves assets. But sometimes the star trade is necessary because the role players can't be aggregated to enough impact.

### 4.4 The recommended portfolios (LAFI-derived primary set)

The prescription is not "do X." It is "consider these combinations of moves, each of which addresses the diagnosed Q4 pathology through a specific path or combination of paths."

Three primary portfolios derived from the LAFI three-paths structure. Each is internally coherent and addresses the Q4 architecture in a specific way.

**Primary Portfolio A: System-first (Path 3 emphasis with light Path 2 support)**
- Coaching emphasis on restoring 2023-24 allocation (iso back to 7-8%, PR-Ball-Handler back to 14-16%).
- Minor catch-and-shoot acquisition at the MLE level.
- Hold Gobert and Randle pending Q8 verdict.
- Bet on Path 3 with light Path 2 support. Preserves cap and assets for 2026-27.
- Best fit if: Q8 finds Gobert and Randle are net positives in the current configuration, and Q0D finds the allocation problem is genuinely coaching-driven (not personnel-locked).

**Primary Portfolio B: Category B acquisition (Path 2 + Path 3 combination)**
- Trade for a high-volume catch-and-shoot wing using draft capital.
- Pair with the coaching changes from Path 3.
- Targeted Path 2 + Path 3 combination. The most defensible combination if Path 1 (Edwards' development) has not yet materialized.
- Best fit if: the diagnosis holds and the front office wants meaningful improvement in 2026-27 without betting on Edwards' tier-leap.

**Primary Portfolio C: Preserve flexibility for Path 1 (minimize moves)**
- Minimize moves while Edwards' trajectory plays out across 2026-27.
- Use system changes (Path 3) to close part of the gap immediately.
- Keep cap flexibility for 2027-28 when Edwards' tier becomes clearer.
- If Edwards reaches Luka/SGA tier in 2026-27, the prescription pivots to "support Category A" (different than supporting Q4 distributed pickup).
- Best fit if: the front office is willing to absorb a transitional season and bet that Path 1 closes the gap structurally.

### 4.5 Alternative portfolios (template moves; weaker evidence backing)

The portfolios below are the original Q5 spec's recommended portfolios, preserved as alternatives. **They are presented with explicit acknowledgment that the LAFI v1 finding (Wolves' Q4 pathology is rare and not well-precedented) weakens the historical evidence for them.** They are template moves derived from observing what other teams have done in roughly similar situations. Use only if the front office believes the Wolves' situation is more conventional than LAFI suggests.

**Alternative Portfolio D: Internal-plus-targeted approach**
- Do not trade Gobert; ride out his deal.
- Sign a stretch shooter at the MLE.
- Trade a future first for a specific secondary creator.
- Bet on Shannon Jr. development.
- Comp logic: this is the standard "tweak around the star and trust internal growth" template. Works when the team is one or two pieces from contention against a known opponent profile.

**Alternative Portfolio E: Reset-and-rebuild-around-Edwards approach**
- Move Gobert for picks and a younger piece (per Q8 verdict if it supports).
- Use the salary relief and picks to acquire a creator.
- Naz becomes the starting 5.
- Tighten the timeline around Ant, McDaniels, Naz.
- Comp logic: the post-deadline Lakers template. Works when the existing personnel mix is structurally unworkable.

**Alternative Portfolio F: All-in approach**
- Trade aggressively for a star secondary creator.
- Likely loses Randle and McDaniels in the deal.
- All-in on the current window with Gobert.
- Comp logic: the 2008 Celtics template. Works when the front office is convinced the contention window is now or never.

The framing in the deliverable should be: "Primary portfolios A, B, C are derived from the LAFI diagnosis. Alternative portfolios D, E, F are template moves. If the diagnosis is correct, the primary portfolios are more directly addressed to the Wolves' specific pathology. If the diagnosis is wrong and the Wolves are in a more conventional failure mode, the alternative portfolios are reasonable. The deliverable defends the primary portfolios but does not foreclose the alternatives."

### 4.6 Portfolio comparison

For each portfolio (primary and alternative), evaluate:
- Probability of success (can we actually get this done?)
- Cost (what do we give up?)
- Time horizon (when does this help?)
- Risk (what's the downside?)
- LAFI-path coverage (which of Paths 1, 2, 3 does this portfolio address?)

Some portfolios dominate others on specific dimensions. The deliverable's job is to make the tradeoffs visible.

## 5. The math

### 5.1 Quantifying gap impact

For each diagnosed gap, estimate the team-level impact of closing it.

This is challenging but doable using a few approaches:

**Counterfactual swap:** If we replace the worst-performing role player at archetype X with a league-average player at archetype X, how does the team's net rating change? Use lineup substitution from Q2.

**Historical comparison:** Among historical cohort comps (from Q0C), how much did teams improve when they closed a specific gap?

**RAPM/EPM estimate:** Use modern impact metrics to estimate the marginal value of a player at the target archetype.

All approaches have limitations. Report ranges, not point estimates.

### 5.2 The expected value calculation

For each portfolio:

```
EV = sum_over_outcomes(P(outcome) * value(outcome))

Where outcomes are:
  - Win championship
  - Reach finals
  - Reach conference finals
  - Reach round 2
  - Round 1 exit
  - Miss playoffs
```

The value assigned to each outcome is a judgment call but a reasonable framework is:
- Championship: 100
- Finals: 25
- Conference finals: 10
- Round 2: 3
- Round 1: 1
- Miss playoffs: 0

These weights reflect that a championship is overwhelmingly the goal; everything else is consolation.

The portfolios can then be ranked by expected value.

### 5.3 Sensitivity to assumptions

The EV calculations are sensitive to the assumed probability of each outcome under each portfolio. These probabilities are educated guesses informed by the cohort analysis but not deterministic.

Run sensitivity analyses: how do the portfolio rankings change if we shift the probabilities by 10% in each direction? If the rankings are stable, the recommendation is robust. If they flip easily, the recommendation is weakly supported and should be presented with that caveat.

**Q1:** Establishes the magnitude of the problem at the four-factors and halfcourt/transition grain. Q5 references Q1 for the team-level decline numbers; LAFI provides the structural diagnosis.

**Q2:** Identifies the specific lineups and players whose performance must be addressed. Feeds Q8 directly. Provides the lineup-level evidence for the Path 3 allocation problem.

**Q3 (narrowed):** PnR coverage decoder. Identifies the specific mechanism by which Edwards is being defended and how the Wolves are bleeding on defense. Feeds Path 1 archetype thresholds (what secondary creator can punish blitzes) and Q8 (Gobert's PnR coverage performance).

**Q4:** Identifies whether the Wolves' Q4 pathology is becoming more common in the league. Feeds the framing of whether the prescription should bet on a stable league environment or on continued evolution.

**Q6 (KAT counterfactual, with LAFI counterfactual addition):** Provides an upper bound on what one major roster move can do. The structural LAFI counterfactual ("would the Wolves have stayed Q1 with KAT?") informs whether the current Q4 placement is fixable by personnel or whether it would have emerged regardless.

**Q7 (Star Comp):** Primary external benchmark when team-level cohort comps are thin. Per LAFI's finding that the Wolves' pathology is rare, Q7's star-level evidence carries the historical weight that Q0C cannot.

**Q8 (Gobert and Randle decisions):** Direct prescriptive input on the two largest specific roster levers. Q5's portfolios reference Q8's verdicts explicitly.

**Q0B (Trajectory):** Edwards trajectory analysis informs Path 1's probability. Other rotation players' trajectories inform when the window closes if Path 1 does not materialize.

**Q0C (Historical Cohort, revised):** Provides base rates with LAFI similarity weighting. Lower confidence than originally planned because the Wolves' Q4 pathology has few clean cohort matches.

**Q0D (Coaching System):** Tests the Path 3 hypothesis directly. If Q0D finds the allocation shift is coaching-driven, Path 3 is more credible. If personnel-locked, Path 3's ceiling drops.

The prescription explicitly references these findings, not just states recommendations from thin air.

## 9. What I'm worried about

**Hand-waving on probabilities.** The EV calculations depend on assumed probabilities that are educated guesses. It's tempting to make up numbers that support a preferred conclusion. Be honest. Document the basis for each probability. Run sensitivity analyses.

**Names creeping into the body.** The temptation to write "we should sign John Smith" instead of "we should sign a player matching the secondary creator archetype" is strong. Resist it. Names go in appendices.

**Over-confident prescriptions.** No one can predict NBA player trajectories or team chemistry perfectly. The prescription should be probabilistic and tentative. Frame as "these moves would plausibly help" rather than "these moves will work."

**Cap and rule changes.** The CBA has nuance and the league occasionally introduces new rules. Some recommendations may be more constrained than they appear. Stay current on the rules.

**Selection bias in historical analogues.** The historical cohort tells us what has worked, but successful teams' moves were partly luck. A move that worked for the 2019 Nuggets might not work for the 2026 Wolves even if the situation looks similar.

**Forcing historical analogies that do not fit.** LAFI v1 established that the Wolves' Q4 pathology is rare. The temptation when historical comps are thin will be to overfit to whatever weak comps exist (Cleveland in Mitchell-Garland years, Mavs in pre-Kyrie years, etc.) and dress them up as stronger evidence than they are. Resist. If Q0C and Q7 produce thin evidence, present it as thin rather than as strong. The first-principles principle in Section 1.3 exists specifically to prevent this failure mode.

**Underweighting Path 3 because it is not an acquisition.** Path 3 (system change) is the cheapest path and the only one that does not depend on roster moves or player development. The temptation will be to under-emphasize it because acquisition-driven prescriptions feel more "real" to readers. The deliverable should give Path 3 weight proportional to its expected impact, not proportional to how concrete it sounds.

**Q8's role in the prescription.** Q8's specific Gobert and Randle verdicts will dominate front office attention. The prescription must integrate Q8 honestly even when its findings complicate the path framework. If Q8 finds Gobert is worth keeping despite Path 3 implications, the prescription must hold both findings.

## 10. Success criteria

**Minimum viable:** A clean gap inventory, defined archetypes, a realistic cap assessment, and at least two coherent portfolios.

**Strong:** Three to four portfolios with quantified expected values, transparent assumptions, and supported by direct reference to the diagnostic findings. Names appendices for each archetype.

**Stretch:** The prescription identifies a specific portfolio or move that the Wolves front office isn't currently considering and that has a defensible quantitative case. This is the version that earns this project meaningful consideration.
