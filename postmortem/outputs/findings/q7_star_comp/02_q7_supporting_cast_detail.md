# Q7 expansion: Supporting cast detail + efficiency edge sharpening

**Date:** 2026-05-18
**Status:** Addendum to `01_q7_v1_findings.md`. Sharpens two pieces flagged by Bobby's data scientist: (1) the efficiency edge framing should be load-bearing, not a footnote, and (2) the supporting cast principle needs concrete named-player templates, not just abstract archetype claims.

## 1. The efficiency edge (sharpened)

The Q7 v1 finding noted Edwards' TS% of 0.617 was the highest in the comp pool. This addendum frames the efficiency edge as load-bearing for the tier-leap probability estimate, not a footnote.

### 1.1 The efficiency gap at age 24

| Player | Age-24 TS% | Age-24 Usage Proxy |
|---|---|---|
| **Anthony Edwards** | **0.617** | 0.756 |
| Devin Booker | 0.587 | 0.745 |
| Jaylen Brown | 0.586 | 0.701 |
| Zach LaVine | 0.568 | 0.755 |
| Donovan Mitchell | 0.569 | 0.788 |

Edwards is **3.0 to 4.9 TS% points** more efficient than his four closest similarity comps at the same age, while operating at similar or slightly higher usage. This is meaningful, not marginal.

### 1.2 What the efficiency gap implies

In NBA terms, 3 TS% points at high usage is approximately equivalent to a tier-upgrade on net offensive impact. The standard rule of thumb: each TS% point at 25+ ppg adds roughly 0.5 ppg of efficient scoring or removes 0.5 ppg of cost. Edwards' 3-5 point gap relative to his closest comps translates to roughly +1.5 to +2.5 ppg of net efficient production at the same usage, OR the ability to absorb 4-6% more usage without efficiency loss.

**The structural meaning:** the historical plateau pattern of Edwards' closest comps was partly driven by their hitting an efficiency ceiling at high usage. Brown, Booker, LaVine, and Mitchell all sustained 0.57-0.59 TS% as their usage scaled. They couldn't push usage further without efficiency cost; they couldn't push efficiency further at their volume.

**Edwards may not have hit that ceiling.** His 0.617 TS% at age 24 is at the level the MVP-tier historical comps reached at their PEAK, not at their age-24 baselines. Compare:

| Player | Age-24 TS% | Career peak TS% | Years to peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kobe | 0.550 | 0.580 (age 27, 2005-06) | 3 |
| Wade | 0.577 | 0.588 (age 26, 2008-09) | 2 |
| Iverson | 0.496 | 0.567 (age 30) | 6 |
| Westbrook | 0.594 | 0.614 (age 27 MVP year) | 3 |
| **Edwards** | **0.617** | **0.617 (already here)** | **0** |

Edwards is already at the efficiency level the MVP-tier historical comps had to develop into over multiple seasons. His tier-leap doesn't require an efficiency leap; it requires sustaining efficiency at higher volume + continued playmaking development.

### 1.3 Adjusted tier-leap probability

The Q7 v1 estimate of 20-30% was anchored on the closest-comp plateau pattern. The efficiency edge argues for the higher end of the range, plausibly higher.

**Updated probability bands:**

- **Modal outcome (45-55%):** Edwards plateaus at his current high-tier (All-NBA selections, top-8-12 league impact). Down from v1's 50-60% because the efficiency edge reduces the risk of efficiency-ceiling plateau.
- **Tier-leap (25-35%):** Edwards becomes MVP-contender (top-5 league impact, championship favorite under right conditions). Up from v1's 20-30% because the efficiency edge gives him room to scale usage without efficiency loss.
- **Decline (10-15%):** Edwards regresses due to injury or shooting regression. Stable from v1.

**The 25-35% range is the more honest framing.** The efficiency edge is real and meaningful, but it's a single dimension. Other factors (defensive limitations, supporting cast quality, playoff variance) still pull toward the modal plateau outcome.

### 1.4 What raises and lowers the tier-leap probability further

**Raises (toward 35-40%):**
- Edwards sustains 0.617 TS% as usage scales to 30%+
- Adds 1-2 ppg in playmaking development without losing efficiency
- The team adds the skilled secondary creator (per Section 2)
- Stays healthy

**Lowers (toward 20-25%):**
- TS% regresses toward his comp set average (0.57-0.59) as defenses scheme harder against him
- Defensive RAPM stays neutral (no MVP-tier comp had elite defense, but most had defensible defense)
- The secondary creator gap remains unfilled
- Injury issues recur

## 2. The supporting cast principle: concrete named-player templates

The Q7 v1 finding identified the consistent missing piece for MVP-tier supporting casts as "a skilled secondary creator." This expansion makes the principle concrete by detailing each MVP-tier historical comp's championship roster (or, for non-champions, what was missing).

### 2.1 Kobe Bryant: the 2009-2010 championship template

**The 2009 NBA Champion Lakers' supporting cast around Kobe:**
- **Pau Gasol (the skilled secondary creator):** 7-foot skilled big with elite passing, mid-range shooting, post scoring. Could initiate offense from the high post or elbow. Made Kobe's job easier by absorbing defensive attention and creating off the catch.
- **Lamar Odom (the versatile forward/secondary creator):** 6'10" multi-positional, point-forward skills, could run pick-and-roll as ball-handler from the wing position. Bench creator who let Kobe rest without offensive collapse.
- **Trevor Ariza (the multi-positional wing defender):** 3-and-D wing who could guard 2-4 positions, allowed Kobe to roam defensively.
- **Andrew Bynum (the defensive anchor / interior scorer):** Rim protection plus secondary post scoring.
- **Derek Fisher (the veteran point guard):** Game-management, clutch shooting, off-ball at age 34.

**The secondary creator distillation:** Pau Gasol and Lamar Odom collectively provided what no single Wolves player provides today. Skilled passing big who can run offense (Gasol) plus a versatile forward who can initiate (Odom). The Wolves have neither archetype on the current roster.

**For 2010, the cast stayed essentially the same** (Ron Artest replaced Ariza for more defense). Continuity plus secondary creation plus rim protection was the championship formula.

### 2.2 Dwyane Wade: the 2006 vs 2012-13 templates (two different paths)

Wade won championships in two distinct supporting-cast structures. This is informative because it shows the secondary creator principle has multiple valid forms.

**2006 NBA Champion Heat (Wade as primary, Shaq as anchor):**
- **Shaquille O'Neal (the dominant defensive anchor + interior force):** Not a "secondary creator" in the modern sense, but his gravity created space for Wade's drives. Shaq's defense and rebounding created the runway.
- **Antoine Walker (the versatile forward with playmaking):** Could initiate from the wing, shoot threes, post up smaller defenders. The closest thing to a secondary creator on this team.
- **Gary Payton (the veteran point guard at age 38):** Game-management, clutch ball-handling, defensive savvy.
- **Udonis Haslem (the role-player big):** Defense, rebounding, mid-range shooter.
- **Jason Williams (the floor-spacing PG):** Three-point shooting, ball-handling.

**Secondary creator analysis:** Walker provided some secondary creation but at lower skill level than Pau Gasol. The 2006 Heat's path was more about Shaq's dominance and Wade's individual brilliance than about Pau-tier secondary creation. **This is a different template: dominant defensive anchor + role-player wings.** The Wolves have Gobert as the analog to Shaq's anchor role.

**2012-13 NBA Champion Heat (Wade as secondary star alongside LeBron):**
- **LeBron James (the primary star AND the team's secondary creator when Wade was on-ball):** This is the unique case. The Heat had two MVP-tier players who could each be the primary AND secondary creator depending on game state.
- **Chris Bosh (the stretch big secondary creator):** Mid-range shooter, post scorer, pick-and-pop, occasional ball-handling. Pure stretch 5 with creation.
- **Mario Chalmers (the spot-up PG):** Catch-and-shoot, low-usage facilitator.
- **Shane Battier / Mike Miller / Ray Allen (3-and-D wings + Allen as elite Category B):** Multi-positional defenders who could shoot.
- **Norris Cole / Mario Chalmers depth.**

**Secondary creator analysis:** LeBron is the most extreme version of the secondary creator archetype - he's both primary and secondary. Bosh's role at the 5 added stretch-shooting plus secondary scoring. **This is the "second star + stretch big" template.** This is harder to replicate (requires acquiring a Top-5 player) but worth noting because Wade's tier-leap to championship MVP came with this supporting cast.

### 2.3 Allen Iverson: the 2001 Finals appearance (cautionary case - never won)

**The 2000-01 Finals 76ers (lost to Lakers 4-1):**
- **Dikembe Mutombo (the dominant defensive anchor):** Multiple DPOY, league-leading rebounder. Gave Iverson the defensive runway.
- **Eric Snow (the veteran point guard):** Game-management, low-usage facilitator, defensive savvy.
- **Aaron McKie (the secondary scorer/playmaker):** Sixth Man of the Year, secondary creation but at modest skill level.
- **Tyrone Hill / Theo Ratliff (the role-player bigs):** Defense, rebounding, screens.

**Secondary creator analysis:** Mutombo was the defensive anchor (analogous to Gobert today). McKie provided some secondary creation but not at MVP-tier skill level. Snow was a glue-guy PG. **The 76ers had NO skilled secondary creator at the Pau-Gasol or Chris-Paul tier.** Iverson reached MVP and Finals on individual brilliance + defensive support + role players. He didn't win the championship.

**The lesson:** MVP-tier individual production + defensive anchor + glue PG can reach the Finals but not win the championship without a skilled secondary creator. This is the cautionary case for the Wolves' current roster shape: Edwards (high-tier individual) + Gobert (defensive anchor) + Conley (glue PG, aging) but no secondary creator. The Wolves' current configuration matches the 2001 76ers more than the 2009 Lakers.

### 2.4 Russell Westbrook: the 2017 MVP year (cautionary case - never won)

**The 2016-17 Thunder (Round 1 exit after winning MVP):**
- **Steven Adams (the defensive anchor / screener):** Rim protection, screen-setting, garbage scoring.
- **Victor Oladipo (the secondary scoring guard, modest creation):** Athletic scoring guard with limited primary playmaking.
- **Domantas Sabonis / Enes Kanter (the rim-running bigs):** Pick-and-roll finishers, rebounding.
- **Andre Roberson / Jerami Grant (the wing defenders):** 3-and-D types with limited offensive creation.

**Secondary creator analysis:** None. Oladipo was the closest thing but pre-breakout. **Westbrook reached MVP-tier individual production without a secondary creator and the team made Round 1.** This is the most extreme cautionary case: even MVP-tier solo brilliance doesn't produce deep playoff success without a skilled secondary creator alongside.

### 2.5 Tracy McGrady: never won (cautionary case)

**Best Magic / Rockets teams (never past Round 1 as primary star):**
- **Magic era:** Mike Miller, Darrell Armstrong, T.J. Ford - solid role players but no secondary creator.
- **Rockets era with Yao Ming:** Yao was a skilled big but injury-prone; durability prevented sustained pairing. When healthy together, the team had bench gaps and no third creator.
- **Defensive anchors variable across eras.**

**Secondary creator analysis:** Yao was the closest McGrady had to a secondary creator, but Yao's durability prevented the pairing from translating to playoff success. **McGrady never had the championship-tier supporting cast.** His individual production was MVP-tier; his team-level outcomes were not. **The lesson:** individual stardom alone doesn't produce playoff success.

### 2.6 The principle synthesis

Across the five MVP-tier comps:

**Championships won with skilled secondary creator (the modal path):**
- Kobe 2009-2010 with Pau Gasol + Lamar Odom
- Wade 2012-13 with LeBron + Chris Bosh (the second-star path)

**Championships won with dominant anchor instead of skilled secondary creator (alternative path):**
- Wade 2006 with Shaq (dominant defensive anchor, no skilled secondary creator)

**Finals reached but championship NOT won (no skilled secondary creator):**
- Iverson 2001 (Mutombo as anchor, no secondary creator)

**Never reached Finals as primary star (no skilled secondary creator):**
- McGrady (multiple teams, never had it)
- Westbrook post-Durant (MVP-tier individual production, no co-star)

**The principle:** A skilled secondary creator is the MOST CONSISTENT FEATURE of MVP-tier comps' championship rosters. The one exception (Wade 2006) required a Shaq-tier dominant anchor as substitute. Stars who reached MVP-tier individual production WITHOUT either a skilled secondary creator OR a Shaq-tier anchor did not win championships, even when they reached the Finals.

### 2.7 What this template means for the Wolves

The Wolves' current roster shape:
- Edwards (the star) ✓
- Gobert (defensive anchor) ✓
- McDaniels (multi-positional wing defender) ✓
- Naz (stretch big with floor-spacing) ✓ (partial - he's a stretch big but lighter on secondary creation than Bosh tier)
- Conley (veteran point guard, aging) ✓ (partial - he's the veteran PG analog)
- Randle (high-usage scorer) - NOT the Pau-Gasol or Chris-Paul tier secondary creator
- **Skilled secondary creator: GAP**

The Wolves match the Iverson 2001 76ers template more than the Kobe 2009 Lakers template. The roster has the defensive anchor and glue elements but lacks the skilled secondary creator that distinguishes championship-winning MVP-tier supporting casts.

**Without this addition, the Wolves' ceiling is "Finals appearance possible, championship unlikely"** - which is the Iverson 2001 outcome.

**With a Pau-Gasol-tier or Chris-Paul-tier addition, the ceiling becomes championship-realistic** - which is the Kobe 2009 outcome.

### 2.8 The Pau Gasol / Chris Paul / Khris Middleton / Jrue Holiday archetype in 2026 terms

Translating the historical secondary creator archetype to the modern league:

**Production profile:**
- High basketball IQ; can initiate offense from multiple positions
- Career assist rate above 25% (or above 18% if a forward / big)
- Career TS% above 0.55
- Defensive minimum: capable of defending at least one starting position without being a liability

**Modern examples that fit the secondary creator archetype:**
- Kyrie Irving (when healthy): primary creation skill at secondary star price
- Jrue Holiday (the modern Eric Snow but with secondary creation): two-way guard who initiates
- Khris Middleton (the modern Chris Paul lite): wing creator + shooting
- Domantas Sabonis: skilled big who initiates from the post and high post
- Trae Young (younger, less playoff-proven): elite primary creator who would be secondary alongside Edwards
- Tyrese Haliburton: pass-first PG with elite creation
- LaMelo Ball (durability question): primary creator skill, would be secondary alongside Edwards

**Acquisition reality:** Most of these players are franchise pieces on long contracts. The realistic acquisition paths are:
1. Major trade for a player like this (~3-5 firsts + significant rotation outflow per Q5 v2 Section 10 precedent prices)
2. Free agency in 2027 if any of them become available (low probability)
3. Internal development of a current Wolves piece into this archetype (very low probability; the team doesn't have a young guard with this profile)

**The Q5 v3 prescription must engage with this acquisition reality.** A Category B catch-and-shoot wing at MLE is the realistic 2026-27 move. The skilled secondary creator is the 2027-reset move (per Portfolio C in Q5 v2) or the major-trade move (per Portfolio B with elevated target).
