## Executive summary

The Wolves' 2025-26 playoff loss to the Spurs was the latest in a pattern: Connelly-era roster builds that specialize against the previous year's playoff problem and get exposed by the next year's different problem. The 2022 Gobert trade built to beat the Nuggets (and did, in 2024 WCF). The 2024 KAT-to-Randle trade responded to the Mavericks small-ball loss. The 2025-26 roster was then exposed by the Spurs' switch-everything-with-rim-protection scheme. Each iteration solves the immediate problem and creates the next vulnerability.

**The recommendation is to break the pattern. Architect for matchup versatility, not opponent specialization.** The Wolves need viable paths against multiple scheme classes (switch-everything, small-ball, blitz-heavy, transition-heavy), not maximum optimization against the most recent loss.

**Concrete prescription (Portfolio A as default):**
- Acquire one multi-positional Category B wing at MLE tier (4+ catch-and-shoot 3PA at 37%+ with movement off-ball AND positional flexibility on defense AND occasional PnR/playmaking ability). The archetype is more demanding than a pure catch-and-shoot specialist.
- Expand Jaden McDaniels' Category B role from current 2.2 to 4+ catch-and-shoot 3PA per game (test in first 20-30 games of 2026-27)
- Restore Gobert's PR-Roll-Man volume and Edwards' PR-Ball-Handler share through coaching emphasis
- Protect DiVincenzo's Achilles recovery; plan around summer 2027 re-sign priority
- Preserve the 2027 cap reset (Gobert and Randle 2027-28 non-guaranteed years) as a strategic asset

**Explicit non-prescriptions:**
- NOT trading Gobert (positive RAPM, intact defense, non-guaranteed 2027-28 gives free optionality)
- NOT trading for Giannis Antetokounmpo or Kevin Durant (diagnostic doesn't point at superstar acquisition; trade math is unfavorable; 2027 reset is the realistic path if pursued)
- NOT architecting specifically against any one opponent or opponent type (the matchup specialization trap)
- NOT roster overhaul (Edwards is the franchise; the supporting cast needs adjustment, not wholesale change)

**The Randle disposition is conditional.** RAPM essentially neutral (-0.07 in 2025-26-only); salary surplus is the most negative on the roster (-2.57 vs implied tier threshold). Trade ONLY if a clear multi-positional Category B replacement is available at his salary slot OR if cap relief enables multiple Category B additions that beat his marginal value in aggregate.

**Three portfolios are presented (A default, B targeted upgrade, C preserve 2027 flexibility), now scored on five dimensions including matchup versatility.**

## 1. The diagnosis

### 1.1 The mechanism (Q1, Q2, Q3)

The Wolves' 2025-26 playoff offensive collapse was statistically distinguishable from league norms at two specific places: off 3PA rate (-0.078, CI [-0.104, -0.054]) and off eFG (-0.065, CI [-0.087, -0.040]). Both CIs exclude zero.

The collapse was Spurs-specific. The team's most-played playoff lineup configuration (Gobert+Randle, Naz off) was net -13.26 over 204 minutes in 2025-26 PO, but +4.57 over 1348 minutes in 2025-26 RS. The yoy lineup comparison showed the same pairing was +5.71 in 2024-25 RS. **The configuration wasn't structurally bad; it broke against the Spurs specifically.**

The Spurs' Wembanyama-anchored switch-everything scheme produced a specific shot diet shift for Edwards:
- 3PA per game: 8.43 (RS) → **5.33** (vs SAS), -37%
- Average shot distance: 15.29 ft → **13.24 ft**, -2.0 feet
- Catch-and-shoot share: 13.8% → **6.2%**, less than half
- 11-16 ft floater zone share: 14.6% → **21.2%**, +6.6 pp
- 23-25 ft 3-point zone share: 25.8% → **16.8%**, -9.0 pp

Edwards adapted individually (46.9% FG on the shorter diet vs RS 48.9%). The team's secondary creation couldn't convert his kickouts.

### 1.2 The architectural context (LAFI v1, Q0D)

The Wolves run a Q4 distributed pickup offense (LAFI v1 classification): off-ball motion held flat at 10-11% across all five Edwards-era seasons; iso rose from 7-8% to 9.6% by 2025-26; PR-Ball-Handler dropped from 14-16% to 13.1%. The system shifted toward iso/transition over time.

But the impact of the allocation shift is small. Restoring 2023-24 allocation with current personnel would gain only **+0.35 ORtg points per 100 possessions** per Q0D analysis. Well below the +2-point threshold for system change as a standalone lever.

Targeted role restorations are higher-leverage: Gobert's PR-Roll-Man volume dropped 41% YoY (188 → 111 possessions; PPP intact at 1.27). Restoring would add +0.6 to +1.0 ORtg concentrated in his minutes. Edwards' PR-Ball-Handler share dropped 10 pp from 2024-25 to 2025-26 (33.3% → 23.5%); restoring would recover 2-3 catch-and-shoot 3PA per game team-wide.

## 2. The prescription framework

### 2.1 One primary path

**"Build the personnel context Edwards needs."**

This consolidates the original Q5 spec's Paths 1 and 2. The diagnostic showed Edwards' development bottleneck isn't his individual skill (offensive RAPM +3.97); it's the supporting cast that converts his kickouts when defenses scheme against him. Path 1 effectively merges into Path 2.

### 2.2 Two supporting elements

**Supporting element 1: System restoration (enabler).** Restore Gobert's PR-Roll-Man volume and Edwards' PR-Ball-Handler share through coaching emphasis. Worth 1-2 ORtg points concentrated.

**Supporting element 2: Conditional Randle disposition.** Trade Randle ONLY if a clear multi-positional Category B replacement is available at his salary slot, OR if cap relief enables multiple Category B additions in aggregate.

### 2.3 What we are explicitly NOT prescribing

- **NOT trading Gobert.** Positive RAPM, intact defense, non-guaranteed 2027-28 gives free optionality. The case for moving him is weak.
- **NOT a roster overhaul.** Edwards is the franchise. The diagnostic shows the supporting cast needs adjustment, not wholesale change.
- **NOT trading for a third superstar (Giannis, Durant, etc.).** Diagnostic doesn't point at superstar acquisition as the bottleneck. Trade math is unfavorable. See Section 11.
- **NOT betting on Edwards' tier-leap to fix everything.** His offensive RAPM is already high; his development is genuine but not the primary lever.
- **NOT architecting specifically against any one opponent or opponent type.** This is the matchup-specialization trap. The Wolves' last three roster constructions have each been built to address the previous year's playoff problem. The pattern is reactive specialization. The recommendation is to break it (Section 8).

## 3. The primary path: build the personnel context Edwards needs

### 3.1 The Category B gap quantification

DiVincenzo in 2025-26 RS: 6.05 catch-and-shoot 3PA/game at 38.3% on 496 attempts across 82 games (elite Category B). Internal options in his absence:

- Naz Reid: 4.47/g at 38.1% (he plays the 5, not wing)
- McDaniels: 2.22/g at 45.1% (high accuracy, low volume, expandable)
- Conley: 2.00/g at 38.9% (aging, declining)
- Edwards: 2.28/g at 49.6% (primary creator)

**The gap:** ~3-4 catch-and-shoot 3PA per game of high-accuracy wing production. McDaniels expansion closes 1-2; external acquisition needs 2-3.

### 3.2 The Category B archetype target (multi-positionality emphasized)

The v1 archetype emphasized switch-everything-resistance. The v2 archetype emphasizes **multi-positionality** because matchup versatility (Section 8) requires the new piece to address multiple threat classes, not just the Spurs/OKC class.

**Production minimum:**
- 4+ catch-and-shoot 3PA per game at 37%+ accuracy
- Capable of running through screens and relocating (movement off-ball)
- Closeout-attack ability (drive to floater or kick when run off the line)

**Multi-positionality requirements (new in v2):**
- Defensive: can defend 1-3 positions, not just one. The current archetype was vague on this; v2 requires switchability.
- Offensive role: can also handle occasional secondary playmaking (PnR ball-handler in spot situations) and off-ball cuts. Not a pure spot-up specialist.
- Pace flexibility: not slow-only or fast-only. The team's transition usage rose in 2025-26; the new piece needs to play at multiple paces.

**Age and contract:** Under 32, on a movable contract or signable at MLE-tier (~$14M)

**Implication:** the v2 archetype is harder to find than the v1 archetype. Some MLE-tier wings would have qualified for v1 but won't qualify for v2 because they lack the defensive switchability or offensive versatility. The trade-off is acquiring a player who addresses multiple scheme threats, not just one.

### 3.3 The secondary creator question

Edwards' individual adaptation to the Spurs scheme was fine. The team's lack of secondary creation was the problem. A secondary creator would help but is harder to acquire than Category B specialists.

**Production minimum:** PR-Ball-Handler PPP > 0.95 vs blitz/aggressive coverage, 35%+ 3P on 4+ attempts per game, 18-24% usage rate.

**Cost reality:** Secondary creators at this profile typically cost $20M+ AAV.

**Recommended approach:** Opportunistic. Pursue if a clear target emerges at the right cost. Otherwise prioritize multi-positional Category B which is easier to acquire and addresses the same diagnostic gap (giving Edwards options to pass to) plus contributes to matchup versatility.

### 3.4 The McDaniels Category B expansion

McDaniels' 45.1% catch-and-shoot accuracy on 2.22 attempts per game is the strongest signal that the Category B gap can be partially closed internally. **Coaching emphasis on getting him 4+ catch-and-shoot 3PA per game would:**

- Partially close the Category B gap internally
- Improve his salary-efficiency picture
- Set up clean 2027-28 evaluation
- Add positional flexibility (he's a defensive wing who would now also be an offensive threat from beyond)

**The risk:** Small-sample 45.1% accuracy may regress when volume scales. If he sustains 38%+ on expanded volume, the gap is partially closed. If he regresses to 33-35%, internal expansion is less valuable.

**Recommended approach:** test in first 20-30 games of 2026-27. Adjust external acquisition urgency based on his accuracy at expanded volume.

### 3.5 The system restoration prescriptions

**Gobert PR-Roll-Man volume restoration.** From 2025-26's 111 possessions back toward 2023-24's 188. The action is in the playbook; the calling has shifted. Restoring it adds 50-77 high-PPP possessions (1.27 PPP) concentrated in Gobert's minutes. Worth +0.6 to +1.0 ORtg points concentrated.

**Edwards PR-Ball-Handler share restoration.** From 2025-26's 23.5% back toward 2024-25's 33.3%. The shift reduced kickout three opportunities team-wide. Restoring would recover 2-3 catch-and-shoot 3PA per game.

**Implementation:** Coaching emphasis prescription, framed as "system, not the man." The current coaching staff can execute. The political sensitivity argues for the system framing.

Randle has a player option for 2026-27 at $33.3M. His 2027-28 ($35.8M) is non-guaranteed.

**Branch A: Randle opts in (most likely).**

- **A1: Keep, deploy correctly.** Maximize Edwards+Randle (+2.68 RS) and Gobert+Randle (+4.57 RS) minutes. Minimize Edwards-OFF Randle-ON cohort. Accept salary-vs-production gap.

- **A3: Cap relief trade.** Trade for expiring contracts plus picks. Bets on the 2027 reset being a major asset.

**Branch B: Randle opts out (less likely).** Team has $30.9M cap space.
- Use on Category B + secondary creator combination
- Cleanest scenario for executing the primary path

### 4.2 The honest framing

Randle is not the problem. His 2025-26-only RAPM is neutral. His pairings with Edwards and Gobert are positive. **He is expensive for what he provides** but he is not destroying value. The trade case rests on opportunity cost, not on him being a bad player.

If the realistic market doesn't produce a clear multi-positional Category B upgrade or meaningful cap flexibility, the default is Sub-branch A1.

## 7. The Spurs replicability question

OKC has the league's best defensive rating (106.6) and rim protection (60.1% opp rim FG%, league best). Multiple Western Conference teams have architectural elements that could partially replicate the Spurs' scheme.

**This sharpens what kind of Category B target the team needs.** A standstill spot-up shooter doesn't help against switch-everything schemes (defenses close out hard and run shooters off the line). The Category B target needs movement off-ball, ability to relocate, and closeout-attack ability.

**Wembanyama's specific versatility is unique** (he can BOTH rim-protect from the floater zone AND switch onto guards). Most teams can do half the scheme. OKC's Holmgren can do most of it.

## 8. Matchup versatility as the roster construction principle

**This is the meta-principle that guides the specific prescriptions in earlier sections.**

### 8.1 The historical pattern: reactive specialization

The Wolves' Connelly-era roster construction has followed a pattern of specializing against the previous year's playoff problem:

**2022 offseason:** Connelly trades for Gobert. The thesis: rim protection plus length to neutralize the Nuggets' interior scoring and harass shooters. This was built to beat the team that won the 2022-23 championship.

**2023-24:** The bet pays off. The Wolves beat Denver in 7 in the WCF. The roster does what it was designed to do.

**2024 WCF:** The Mavericks expose the design's vulnerability. Small ball, Luka and Kyrie spacing, Lively as a roll threat. The Wolves' bigger lineups get hunted. The roster that beat Denver couldn't beat Dallas.

**Summer 2024:** KAT-to-Randle trade. Randle is a more conventional power forward partly responsive to what Dallas exposed. The team gains some flexibility but disrupts the offensive architecture (LAFI's core finding: the Q4 distributed pickup pathology emerged here).

**2024-25:** WCF again (different opponent loss).

**2025-26:** The Spurs' Wembanyama-anchored switch-everything scheme exposes the new build's vulnerabilities. The Wolves are back to "the previous year's playoff matchup exposed a problem that this year's roster doesn't solve."

**The pattern is reactive specialization.** Each iteration solves the immediate problem and creates the next vulnerability.

### 8.2 The principle: architect for matchup versatility, not specialization

**Reactive specialization is a trap.** Every NBA roster will eventually face a matchup it wasn't built for, because every roster will face a different opponent in successive playoff rounds and seasons. Building specifically to beat the most recent loss optimizes for a problem that may not recur and ignores problems that will.

**The principle:** architect to give the team viable paths against any major scheme class. Not "architect for the modal case." The Wolves should be able to compete against:

- **Switch-everything with rim protection** (Spurs, OKC, Boston with healthy Porzingis). The current Category B addition partially addresses this.
- **Small-ball spacing** (the 2024 Mavericks pattern; many modern teams). The Wolves' two-big lineups get hunted on switches. Versatility requires either a defensively-switchable 5 (Naz at the 5 with appropriate guards) or a defensively-switchable 4 (a 4 who can guard wings in switches).
- **Blitz/aggressive PnR coverage.** Defenses sending two on Edwards. Q3 showed the Wolves can't reliably convert this kind of pressure. Category B helps but doesn't fully solve; needs secondary creation that can punish doubles.
- **Transition-heavy pace.** Teams that push pace force the Wolves' bigger lineups into transition defense. The current bigs (Gobert, Randle, Naz) create lineup limits in transition.

**What versatility requires:**

1. **Multiple defensive lineup configurations.** Switch-heavy small ball (Naz at 5, McDaniels at 4, a defensively-versatile 4 next to him) AND rim-protection traditional (Gobert at 5, Randle at 4). The current roster has both options conceptually; the question is whether the right players exist for each.

2. **Multiple offensive engine options.**
   - Edwards as primary creator (current default)
   - Edwards as off-ball threat with secondary creator (current weakness)
   - DiVincenzo as movement shooter pulling defenders (when healthy)
   - McDaniels as cutter/relocator (underused)

3. **Multiple pace profiles.** Half-court grinding (current strength) AND transition-heavy (current weakness; the team's transition share rose in 2025-26 but its half-court remained the primary mode).

### 8.3 What versatility costs

Versatility is more demanding than specialization. The same cap dollars buy less specialized excellence and more positional/scheme flexibility. The trade-offs:

- A pure catch-and-shoot Category B specialist at MLE is easier to find than a multi-positional Category B wing
- A switchable defender who also shoots is rarer (and more expensive) than a switchable defender or a shooter alone
- A multi-positional 4 (stretch shooting + perimeter defense + occasional secondary playmaking) commands a higher salary than a traditional stretch 4

**Versatility is a multi-year project.** The Wolves can take steps toward it in summer 2026 (one multi-positional Category B addition + McDaniels role expansion). They can take larger steps in summer 2027 if the reset is executed. Full versatility against all major scheme classes requires 2-3 years of consistent roster construction discipline.

### 8.4 Why this principle matters more than opponent-specific design

A front office reader looking at the Q5 v1 deliverable might ask: "What if we don't play OKC?" The v1 answer was "OKC is the modal case; building for the modal case provides broad protection." That's defensible but it still implies a target opponent.

**The v2 answer:** "We're not architecting for any specific opponent. We're architecting for the team to have viable paths against any major scheme class, because the playoff opponent in any given year is uncertain and the league evolves."

This is a more demanding standard. It's also a more defensible recommendation because it doesn't depend on the bracket cooperating. A versatile roster competes against multiple opponent types. A specialized roster needs the bracket to deliver favorable matchups.

### 8.5 Why this principle is supported by the data

The diagnostic findings line up with the versatility framing:

- **Q3 showed the Spurs' specific scheme suppressed Edwards' threes.** But the broader category of switch-everything-with-rim-protection schemes is replicable; multiple teams have elements of it. Architecting for "anti-Spurs specifically" addresses a class of opponents, not a single one.

- **Q2 yoy showed the Gobert+Randle pairing's RS performance is fine; the playoff collapse was Spurs-specific.** The lineup configurations the Wolves already have can work; the question is whether they have answers to multiple opponent types or just to some.

- **Q8 + RAPM showed DiVincenzo, Gobert, McDaniels are all positive contributors in different ways.** The team's role-player depth is real. The versatility limitation is in scheme-flexibility, not in talent.

- **Q0D showed allocation restoration alone has small impact (+0.35 ORtg).** The Wolves can't system-restore their way to versatility. They need personnel additions that contribute versatility to the roster.

### 8.6 The explicit non-prescription

**We are NOT recommending architecting specifically against any one opponent or opponent type.** This is the matchup-specialization trap that the Connelly-era pattern has fallen into. The Q5 v2 prescription is framed against this trap.

## 9. The three portfolios (re-evaluated on versatility)

### Portfolio A: "Build the supporting cast" (DEFAULT)

**Personnel:**
- Acquire one multi-positional Category B wing at MLE tier (per Section 3.2)
- Expand McDaniels' Category B role
- Test in first 20-30 games of 2026-27

**System:**
- Coaching emphasis on PR-Ball-Handler and PR-Roll-Man restoration
- Worth 1-2 ORtg concentrated

**Player decisions:**
- Keep Gobert through 2026-27
- Keep Randle; deploy correctly; reassess at trade deadline
- Protect DiVincenzo recovery

**2027 plan:**
- Preserve flexibility for 2027 reset

**Versatility added:** MODERATE. The multi-positional Category B addition adds defensive switchability and offensive role-flexibility. Combined with McDaniels' expanded role, the team's wing rotation becomes more versatile. The big rotation (Gobert/Randle/Naz) stays the same, which limits versatility against small-ball threats.

**Expected impact:** +2 to +4 ORtg points. Championship probability change: +2 to +4 percentage points.

### Portfolio B: "Targeted upgrade"

**Differs from A:** Trade Randle for a multi-positional Category B-fitting stretch four (defense + shooting + occasional playmaking).

**Same as A:** System restoration, Gobert keep, DiVincenzo recovery, McDaniels expansion.

**Versatility added:** HIGHER. Replacing Randle (traditional 4 with limited switchability) with a multi-positional 4 (stretch shooting + perimeter defense + lineup flexibility) adds:
- Small-ball compatibility (the new 4 can play next to Naz at 5 in switchable small-ball)
- Improved Gobert+4 spacing (a true stretch 4 next to Gobert creates more roll space)
- Possible offensive engine option (a 4 with some playmaking can run secondary actions)

**Expected impact:** +3 to +6 ORtg points if execution lands. Championship probability change: +5 to +10 percentage points.

**Risk:** Execution. Randle market may not produce clear multi-positional upgrade. The bar is higher than v1's framing because v2 requires versatility, not just better production.

### Portfolio C: "Preserve 2027 flexibility"

**Personnel:**
- Sign multi-positional Category B at MLE on a 1-year deal
- Do not pursue trades unless clearly value-additive

**System:** Same as A and B.

**Player decisions:**
- Same as A (keep Gobert, Randle, DiVincenzo)
- Plan to potentially waive Gobert 2027-28 if performance declines

**2027 plan:**
- Major free-agent or trade pursuit using ~$80M cap room
- Re-sign DiVincenzo aggressively if proves out
- Possibly pursue superstar trade if right opportunity (Q9)
- Architect for versatility from the ground up using cap room

**Versatility added:** HIGHEST in the long run. The 2027 reset enables the team to design for versatility comprehensively. Pick a multi-positional 4 + a switchable secondary creator + retain DiVincenzo as the Category B anchor + decide on Gobert based on his 2026-27 performance. The 2027 cap room enables 3-4 strategic acquisitions rather than 1-2.

**Expected impact:** 2026-27 +1 to +3 ORtg points (most conservative). 2027-28 step-change potential.

**Risk:** Edwards' competitive window may be closing if team isn't competitive in 2026-27. The 2027 reset is real only if executed; bad 2027 decisions undo the strategic asset.

## 10. Why we don't recommend trading for Giannis Antetokounmpo or Kevin Durant (Q9 expansion)

This section exists because Wolves fans, media, and likely the front office will ask the question. The treatment is substantive, not dismissive. The honest answer is that neither trade is recommended given the diagnostic findings, but the engagement has to be real.

### 10.1 The question

Should the Wolves pursue a star acquisition (Giannis or Durant) to address the 2025-26 playoff outcome? This is the question fans, media, and possibly the front office will ask first. The diagnostic-driven prescription in Sections 1-9 doesn't point at superstar acquisition. This section explains why, engages the counter-argument respectfully, and identifies the conditions under which the recommendation would change.

### 10.2 Recent star trade pricing (the comparable set)

To project what a Giannis or Durant trade would cost the Wolves, look at the precedent prices for similar star trades over the last four years.

**Donovan Mitchell to Cleveland (September 2022).** Cleveland gave up Collin Sexton, Lauri Markkanen, Ochai Agbaji, plus three unprotected first-round picks (2025, 2027, 2029) and two pick swaps (2026, 2028). The headline price was effectively three young rotation players plus five first-round assets (3 picks + 2 swaps). Mitchell was 25, two years from a player option, and a top-15 player at acquisition. The Jazz, in rebuild, were the seller.

**Kevin Durant to Phoenix (February 2023).** Phoenix gave up Mikal Bridges, Cameron Johnson, Jae Crowder, four unprotected first-round picks (2023, 2025, 2027, 2029), and a pick swap (2028). Three rotation players plus five first-round assets. Durant was 34, two more years on his contract, and still a top-10 player. The Nets, who had just lost Kyrie, were the seller.

**Damian Lillard to Milwaukee (September 2023).** Milwaukee gave up Jrue Holiday (later flipped to Boston for Brogdon, Robert Williams, picks), Toumani Camara, Grayson Allen, one unprotected first (2029), and two pick swaps (2028, 2030). Lillard was 33, signed long-term, top-15 player at acquisition. Portland in rebuild was the seller.

**Mikal Bridges to New York (June 2024).** Brooklyn received five unprotected first-round picks plus role players. Bridges was 27 at the time, a 3-and-D wing on a team-friendly contract, viewed as a possible third star. Note: five firsts for a "third-star-tier" player, not even a primary star. This sets the floor for star-level acquisition cost.

**Kevin Durant to Houston (mid-2025).** Houston gave up Jalen Green plus picks plus Dillon Brooks. Durant was 36 at the time of the trade. Lower price than the 2023 Suns acquisition because of the age decline. Roughly two rotation pieces plus three first-round assets.

**The pattern across these comps:** primary star acquisitions cost three rotation players + 4-5 first-round assets (picks or swaps) on the asset side, plus salary matching. Younger or higher-tier stars cost more. Older or declining stars cost less but still substantial. There is no "we'll keep our best player and add a star" trade. The acquiring team's best non-star asset goes in the deal.

### 10.3 The Giannis trade math (concretely)

Giannis Antetokounmpo turns 32 in December 2026 (during the 2026-27 season). He has multiple years remaining on his max extension. His RAPM in his peak years (2022-23) was approximately +6 to +8. In the most recent measurement period, he was still a top-5 league-wide impact player.

**Salary matching:** Giannis' 2026-27 salary is approximately $54-56M. To match, the Wolves must send approximately $50M+ in outgoing salary (within standard match rules).

**The Wolves' salary inventory:**
- Edwards: $48.9M (could be the headline outgoing piece + small filler)
- Gobert: $36.5M (older, would partly match)
- Randle: $33.3M if opted in
- McDaniels: $26.2M
- Naz Reid: $23.3M

**The realistic salary structures:**

| Trade structure | Outgoing salary | Asset cost | Edwards stays? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Edwards + filler | ~$50M | Likely 4+ firsts, swaps | NO |
| Gobert + Randle + filler | ~$70M+ | 4-5 firsts, must include rotation depth | YES |
| Randle + McDaniels + Naz + filler | ~$83M+ | Same firsts plus loses 3 starters | YES |

**Asset cost (per precedent):** Five first-round picks (or 3-4 picks + 2 swaps), plus filler players. The Bucks would need this because:
1. They'd be selling a top-3 league asset and need maximum future value
2. Multiple bidders would exist (CLE, MIA, ATL, etc.) creating competitive pricing
3. The 2023 Mitchell and Durant trades set the floor at 5 first-round assets for stars at that tier

**The Wolves' draftable inventory:** First-round picks owed/owned (per public reporting through 2025):
- Wolves picks generally outgoing or controlled through the Gobert trade
- 2027 first owned, with restrictions
- 2029 and beyond mostly available
- Pick swaps available

**The realistic Wolves package for Giannis:**

Option 1 (Wolves keep Edwards):
- Outgoing: Randle, McDaniels, Naz Reid, Beringer, Clark, plus 2027 first, 2029 first, 2031 first, 2033 first, two swaps
- Incoming: Giannis + filler (small contracts to balance)
- **Post-trade Wolves rotation:** Edwards, Gobert, DiVincenzo (recovering), Conley, Dosunmu, Hyland, Phillips, Shannon, Beringer (if retained), plus Giannis + filler

Option 2 (Edwards goes):
- Outgoing: Edwards + Randle + 3 firsts + swaps (Bucks far more interested in this because Edwards is younger)
- Incoming: Giannis + Khris Middleton (if his contract is matched) + filler
- **Post-trade Wolves rotation:** Giannis, Gobert, McDaniels, Naz Reid, DiVincenzo (recovering), Conley, Dosunmu, McDaniels, plus filler

**Both structures gut the Wolves' depth.** The current roster's strength is its depth at multiple positions. Either trade structure trades depth for ceiling.

### 10.4 The Durant trade math (concretely)

Kevin Durant turns 38 in September 2026, ages 39 during 2026-27 season. He's on the back end of his career with one or two more guaranteed-production years and high variance after.

**Salary matching:** Durant's 2026-27 salary is approximately $53-55M (depending on his most recent extension structure).

**Asset cost (per Houston precedent):** Two-three rotation players plus 3 first-round assets, roughly. Lower than Giannis because of age and Durant's likely shorter remaining peak window. The seller (Houston, or wherever he is) would have less leverage because the bidding market is smaller for older stars.

**The realistic Wolves package for Durant:**

Most plausible structure:
- Outgoing: Randle + McDaniels + 3 firsts + swaps
- Incoming: Durant + matching filler
- **Post-trade Wolves rotation:** Edwards, Durant, Gobert, Naz Reid, DiVincenzo (recovering), Conley, Dosunmu, Clark, plus Beringer/Shannon depth

The Durant trade doesn't require Edwards. The Durant price is lower because his trade value is lower. The trade-off is acquiring a 38-year-old star with possibly 2 productive seasons left.

### 10.5 The post-trade roster projection (using Q2 + Q8 framework)

**For the Giannis trade (Wolves keep Edwards):**

The acquired roster's RAPM-weighted offensive ceiling is meaningful but the depth loss is severe. Using the project's 2025-26-only RAPM estimates:

- Edwards (+3.97 off RAPM) + Giannis (estimated +4-5 RAPM in 2026-27 given age curve) = high-end primary creation tandem
- Lose Randle (-0.07), McDaniels (-0.48), Naz Reid (+2.70). Net RAPM lost: roughly +2.2 from the rotation departures
- Net change in RAPM at the top: roughly +4 to +5 (Giannis added minus rotation lost)
- BUT: the depth loss is structural. Bench play with Conley + Dosunmu + Hyland + Phillips is significantly thinner than the current bench

**The lineup-grain estimate:** The starting lineup with Edwards + Giannis + Gobert + (DiVincenzo or McDaniels or Conley) + (the remaining wing) is plausibly +6 to +10 net rating when healthy. Bench minutes drop by 5-8 net rating points compared to current depth. Net effect on 2026-27: small positive in expectation, large variance.

**For the Giannis trade with Edwards going:**

Loses Edwards (+3.97 off RAPM). Adds Giannis (+4-5 RAPM). Net add on the primary creation: +0-1 RAPM at age curves. Plus Khris Middleton if his contract is involved (uncertain RAPM but likely modest positive). The depth picture is similar to Option 1 but the team's identity changes completely.

**For the Durant trade:**

Edwards stays. Lose Randle (-0.07) and McDaniels (-0.48). Add Durant (estimated +2-3 RAPM in 2026-27 given age 38). Net add: roughly +2.5 to +3 RAPM at the top of the rotation. Depth loss is moderate (Randle and McDaniels are not the team's biggest assets but the position depth declines).

**Year-by-year impact projection:**

For Durant:
- 2026-27: +2-4 ORtg, possibly playoff trip improvement, depending on health
- 2027-28: variance widens; Durant could decline materially
- 2028-29: Durant retiring or near-retirement; trade was effectively a 1-2 year window bet

For Giannis (Wolves keep Edwards):
- 2026-27: depends heavily on Giannis health (he's had injury concerns); upside is high ceiling, downside is depth-induced mediocrity if Giannis misses time
- 2027-28: maybe peak window if Giannis healthy; Edwards entering prime
- 2028-29: continued window
- The bet: 3-year competitive window with the new core

For Giannis (Edwards goes):
- The team is now Giannis-led with a different supporting cast
- The 5-year window is built around Giannis (older but more proven) rather than Edwards (younger but less proven)
- The bet is essentially "swap our 25-year-old for the league's best 31-year-old"

### 10.6 The age curve question

Star age curves vary by archetype. Some patterns:

**Giannis-archetype (athletic, drive-and-finish):** Peak production years 26-30. Decline typically gradual through early 30s. Knee health is the key risk variable. Giannis at 32 should still be elite (+5 to +6 RAPM realistic, +7 ceiling if healthy). At 34, the decline begins materially. The window for getting peak production from a Giannis trade is roughly 2026-27 through 2028-29.

**Durant-archetype (skilled, shot-creation):** Late-career production can persist longer than athletic archetypes. Durant at 38 has shown he can still produce at high level when healthy. The risk is durability, not skill decay. The window for Durant is essentially 2026-27 and possibly 2027-28.

**Edwards comparison:** Edwards turns 25 in August 2026. His age curve is just beginning. The Wolves giving up Edwards' next 5+ peak years for any star acquisition is a trade of long-term value for short-term ceiling.

**Critical:** the Wolves' current championship odds rest partly on Edwards being a multi-year asset. Acquiring Giannis or Durant gives up multi-year future for short-term ceiling. The expected value math depends on (a) the probability the new core wins now, and (b) the cost of giving up Edwards' future years.

### 10.7 The realistic probability of trade availability

**Giannis:**
- Bucks have repeatedly signaled commitment to building around Giannis (Holiday trade, Lillard trade, multiple cap commitments)
- Giannis has publicly stated commitment when asked
- Realistic probability he becomes available in 2026: 10-20%, depending on the Bucks' 2025-26 outcome
- Realistic probability he becomes available in 2027 (post-reset year): 25-35% if the Bucks' contention window doesn't materialize
- **Competitive bidding:** if available, 6-8 teams would bid aggressively. The Wolves' offer would need to be competitive among the top suitors.

**Durant:**
- Older players are more commonly traded as their contracts expire
- The team holding Durant (likely Houston or wherever he is in 2026) has lower retention pressure given his age
- Realistic probability of availability in 2026: 30-50%
- Competitive bidding for a 38-year-old Durant is more modest

**The pursue probability is a separate question from availability.** Even if available, the Wolves must conclude the trade is the best use of their assets. Sections 10.3-10.6 suggest the assessment is closer to negative than positive.

### 10.8 The honest answer

**Neither trade is recommended given the diagnostic findings.**

The diagnostic in Sections 1-9 doesn't point at superstar acquisition as the bottleneck. The Wolves' 2025-26 issues were:
- DiVincenzo's Achilles tear (the team's highest-impact player by 2025-26 RAPM)
- The Spurs' specific defensive scheme suppressing Edwards' three-point creation
- The team's lack of secondary off-ball creation

Adding a third superstar doesn't address these specific gaps. A Giannis trade that includes Edwards swaps one star for another, doesn't help with Category B, doesn't help with off-ball creation. A Giannis trade that keeps Edwards severely depletes the supporting cast that the diagnostic identified as the gap. A Durant trade adds a 38-year-old to a roster with championship odds dependent on Edwards' multi-year peak; the math is short-window.

**The Giannis trade is the closer call.** If the team had to make a star move, Giannis at 32 has a better expected production curve than Durant at 38. But the trade math is brutal. Either Edwards goes (sacrificing the franchise's long-term anchor) or the supporting cast is gutted (defeating the diagnostic prescription). Neither version cleanly improves the team's championship odds given the assumptions.

**The Durant trade is more achievable but less impactful.** Durant at 38 likely adds +2-3 RAPM in 2026-27 and variance after. The trade cost (Randle + McDaniels + 3 firsts roughly) is significant but not catastrophic. The honest case for Durant: "buy two years of championship-ceiling ceiling using assets that don't compromise the long-term build." The honest case against: "Durant at 38 isn't a high enough delta over the diagnostic-driven Portfolio A path to justify the asset cost."

**The honest counter-argument the Wolves should engage with respectfully:** the Wolves' offensive ceiling is somewhat capped by Edwards being their single creation engine. Adding a second elite creator (Giannis especially, given his playmaking) raises the offensive ceiling in ways that better personnel around Edwards cannot. **This argument has merit.** A two-star roster has options that a one-star-plus-supporting-cast roster doesn't. The Q5 prescription's response is that the diagnostic specifically showed Edwards' individual creation was good (his FG% against the Spurs was 46.9%); what failed was the supporting cast's conversion of his kickouts. The argument that "another star fixes this" assumes the new star plays the off-ball complementary role that no current Wolves player is filling; in practice, two-star rosters often have both stars wanting on-ball time, which doesn't solve the off-ball Category B gap.

### 10.9 The 2027 reset alternative

The cleanest path for a Wolves superstar pursuit (if the front office decides to pursue) is the 2027 reset (Section 6), not 2026-27 trades.

**By summer 2027:**
- The Wolves have ~$80M cap room if Gobert's and Randle's non-guaranteed years are not exercised
- DiVincenzo is a free agent
- Edwards is approaching his prime
- The 2027 market may have different stars available (contract expirations, league movement)

**The advantage of 2027 over 2026:**
- Cap room enables absorbing a star's salary in trade with less salary outflow
- The Wolves' asset pile (4+ remaining first-round picks owed/owned, plus pick swaps) is preserved
- Edwards is more developed and proven at age 26
- The team has additional information about Gobert's trajectory, DiVincenzo's recovery, McDaniels' role expansion

**The disadvantage:** Giannis and Durant are both older by 2027 (Giannis 32-33, Durant 38-39 at acquisition). The specific stars currently in fan discussion may not be the right 2027 targets. Other stars may emerge.

**The 2027 reset framing for a star pursuit:** if the front office wants to keep open the option of a star acquisition, the path is to execute Portfolio C (preserve flexibility) in 2026-27, then evaluate the star market in summer 2027. The cap room and asset preservation make a 2027 trade meaningfully easier than a 2026 trade.

### 10.10 The recommendation

**Do not pursue Giannis or Durant in 2026.** Execute Portfolio A or B per the v2 prescription. Build the personnel context Edwards needs through targeted Category B acquisition.

**If the front office wants to keep the star pursuit open:** execute Portfolio C in 2026-27, preserve the 2027 cap reset, evaluate the 2027 market when more information is available.

**The framing for fan and media engagement:** the Wolves are not declining the star pursuit because of timidity or lack of ambition. They're declining because the diagnostic shows the team's specific gaps aren't addressed by a third superstar. The path to championship contention is supporting cast around Edwards, not a star swap. This is a substantive analytical position, not a posture.

## 14. The honest framing (versatility lens)

None of this guarantees a championship. The Wolves compete in a deep Western Conference with multiple teams that have defensive architectures capable of giving Edwards-led offenses trouble in different ways. The prescription is "give Edwards the supporting cast that makes him hardest to scheme against in multiple schemes, not just the most recent one."

**The structural takeaway:** the Wolves' 2025-26 problems weren't catastrophic and weren't player failures. They were partly the latest iteration of the Connelly-era reactive specialization pattern. The fix isn't another reaction to the most recent loss. It's a multi-year commitment to building a versatile roster that can compete against multiple scheme classes.

**Three positive-impact contributors (Gobert, DiVincenzo, Edwards) at the individual level.** The supporting cast around them needs adjustment, not overhaul. The 2027 reset is a real strategic asset if intermediate decisions preserve it.

**Edwards is fine.** Gobert is fine. Randle is approximately neutral. DiVincenzo is the question. The team's job is to build the supporting cast that lets Edwards' creation produce points against multiple scheme types, not just the most familiar ones.

**The versatility principle reframes everything.** Each prescription is evaluated not just for "does this fix the Spurs problem" but for "does this make the team more matchup-versatile across multiple scheme classes." Portfolio A is a step toward versatility. Portfolio B is two steps if executed well. Portfolio C is the long-run versatility play.
