# Q5 v4 refinement notes for the website pass

**Date:** 2026-05-18
**Status:** Not a v5 of the prescription. The Q5 v4 central framework is sound; these refinements are at-the-margin sharpenings the data scientist flagged for incorporation during the website-version writing pass. Captured here so they aren't lost between the analytical phase and the public-facing phase.

The v4 deliverable lands its central thesis (architectural ceiling validated by Q0C; prescription is the path out of the cohort; Edwards tier-leap is upside not architectural fix). It rests on the Embiid Sixers and Phoenix Suns comps as load-bearing cautionary evidence. The framings below sharpen how those comps are presented without changing what they demonstrate.

## Refinement 1: The 2021-22 Sixers comp has specific factors beyond architectural pathology

**Current v4 framing (Section 6.4, 6.7, 13.3, 15):** "MVP-candidate talent did not break the architectural ceiling. PHI 2021-22 lost R2."

**The refinement:** The Sixers' 2021-22 season had structural disruptions that contributed to the R2 outcome beyond pure architectural ceiling:

- **Harden was a mid-season acquisition** (traded for Simmons in February 2022). Team chemistry was demonstrably strained. The offensive flow had to integrate a max-tier ball-dominant guard mid-season.
- **Embiid was dealing with physical issues throughout** the season and entered the playoffs with an orbital fracture and concussion suffered in R1 against Toronto. He played R2 in a mask.
- **R2 vs Miami had specific tactical factors:** Heat's switchability + Lowry/Butler aggression on Harden's defensive limitations; Doc Rivers rotation choices; Embiid's facial injury constraining his usage.

**Honest framing for the website:** "The Sixers comp suggests architectural pathology limits ceiling even with MVP-candidate talent, though specific factors (chemistry disruption from mid-season trade, Embiid's facial injury entering R2, tactical matchup with Miami's switching) also contributed to the R2 outcome. The comp is informative directionally but its single-data-point precision should not be overstated."

**Why this matters:** the Embiid Sixers comp is doing a lot of inferential work in v4. Acknowledging the specific factors makes the framing more defensible to a sophisticated reader without weakening the directional finding.

## Refinement 2: The 2023-24 Suns comp has specific factors beyond architectural pathology

**Current v4 framing (Section 6.4, 13.2, 13.3):** "Three All-NBA scorers + same architectural extreme = R1 sweep."

**The refinement:** The Suns' 2023-24 had specific roster construction issues that contributed to the R1 sweep beyond pure architectural ceiling:

- **Beal was injury-plagued and underperformed** his max contract (only 53 games played; PPG and shooting both below career norms).
- **Supporting cast was minimum-salary players** because Beal's contract consumed the cap. Defense was structurally compromised by lack of perimeter defenders.
- **They faced a peak-form Wolves team** with Edwards going supernova (R1 ECF appearance the next round). The sweep was as much about the opponent's quality as about the Suns' pathology.
- **Frank Vogel coaching tenure** was widely viewed as a fit problem; the system wasn't optimized for the talent on hand.

**Honest framing for the website:** "The Suns comp is the cleanest available evidence that adding primary creator talent to this architectural state doesn't break the ceiling. Specific factors (Beal underperformance, supporting cast minimum-salary depth, matchup against peak-form Wolves, coaching system fit questions) contributed to the specific R1 outcome. The directional finding (superstar acquisition doesn't address architectural pathology) holds; the precision of the R1-sweep data point should not be overstated."

**Why this matters:** the Suns comp is load-bearing evidence for the "NOT Giannis/Durant" framing. Acknowledging the specific factors makes the framing more defensible without undermining the central argument.

## Refinement 3: The 25-30% probability has hidden assumptions

**Current v4 framing (Section 10.1, 10.2):** "Probability of avoiding both vulnerable clusters across 4-round championship run: 25-30%."

**The refinement:** The number rests on several assumptions that the website should make visible:

- **Assumption 1: League cluster distribution stays roughly stable.** The 2025-26 cluster distribution is the basis. If the league shifts (more or fewer teams in Cluster 1 or 3), the probability shifts. Recent NBA trends suggest the proportion of high-3PA + elite-rim-protection teams is growing, which would push the Wolves' avoidance probability lower.
- **Assumption 2: Cluster teams are evenly distributed across the bracket.** They're not necessarily. The Western Conference has had a disproportionate share of high-3PA teams. If the Wolves' bracket is WC-heavy, the probability is lower than the league-average calculation suggests.
- **Assumption 3: Wolves' per-cluster performance is consistent.** Small samples per cluster (6 games vs Cluster 1, 5 games vs Cluster 3) make this uncertain. The 25-30% rests on those small samples.

**Honest framing for the website:** "Roughly 25-30% based on (a) historical cluster distribution at 2025-26 levels, (b) assumption that Wolves' per-cluster performance patterns hold, and (c) small-sample uncertainty around specific cluster matchups. The number is directionally informative. The precision should not be overstated."

**Why this matters:** the probability is referenced multiple times as a structural feature. Making the assumptions visible is the credibility-establishing move for analytically literate readers.

## Refinement 4: The Iverson vs Kobe template framing is illustrative not strict

**Current v4 framing (Section 18.2):** "Wolves match the 2001 Sixers' template (defensive anchor + glue PG + high-tier individual scorer). Prescription moves them toward 2009 Lakers' template (defensive anchor + skilled secondary creator + multi-positional wing + glue PG + star guard)."

**The refinement:** The two-template framing is rhetorically powerful but oversimplifies what championship rosters look like. Other championship teams don't fit cleanly:

- **2008 Celtics:** three primary creators (KG, Pierce, Allen). No clear secondary creator in the Pau mold. Won via defense and offensive depth.
- **2019 Raptors:** Kawhi as primary, complementary pieces (Lowry, Gasol, Siakam). No clear secondary creator in the Pau mold. Won via Kawhi's level-up plus defense.
- **2021 Bucks:** Giannis + Middleton + Holiday. Closer to the Kobe template but with different position dynamics (Middleton is the secondary creator analog).
- **2022 Warriors:** Curry + Klay + Wiggins + Draymond. Pace + space + Curry transcendence. Doesn't fit either template cleanly.
- **2023 Nuggets:** Jokic + Murray + Gordon + Pope + Porter. Two-man game centerpiece (Jokic-Murray) plus complementary wings. Different structure.

**Honest framing for the website:** "The Iverson 2001 vs Kobe 2009 comparison is illustrative of the structural difference between 'star carries you' and 'skilled secondary creator unlocks the star.' Other championship rosters don't always fit either template cleanly. The 2008 Celtics, 2019 Raptors, 2022 Warriors, and 2023 Nuggets all won championships with different structural configurations. The prescription's secondary-creator emphasis is grounded in the diagnostic (the Wolves' Q4 pathology + Q7 supporting cast comp set), not in claiming the Kobe template is the only path."

**Why this matters:** the template framing is the deliverable's most memorable conceptual move. Maintaining its rhetorical force while acknowledging that championship rosters take various shapes is the right balance for a serious basketball reader.

## What changes in the website but not in Q5 v4

The Q5 v4 deliverable in the findings folder remains the canonical analytical document. The website pass is where these four refinements get incorporated into the public-facing writing. The website's framing will be:

1. Cite the Embiid Sixers and Phoenix Suns comps as load-bearing evidence with specific-factor caveats
2. Present the 25-30% probability with its assumptions visible
3. Use the Iverson vs Kobe templates as illustrative comparison, not strict empirical pattern

If Phase 3 infrastructure work (full league-wide RAPM, Spotrac integration) eventually produces enough new findings to warrant a Q5 v5, these refinements can be incorporated there. But they don't justify v5 on their own.
