The two-man synergy is small for every duo (-3.8 to +2.8) and it does NOT track success. The
Finals-team success (Doncic-Irving) is -0.7; the canonical fit FAILURE (Young-Murray) is +0.8.
Every duo, including the ones that "failed," ran a FINE offense with both on (116-121). The
offensive "usage collision" that the bull/bear debate centers on is, in the data, a small effect
that did not sink the failed pairings. Their offenses worked; they failed elsewhere.

## What this reframes: the failures were DEFENSIVE / holistic, not offensive

Young-Murray and Booker-Beal did not collapse because two high-usage guards could not share the
ball, their offenses were good. They collapsed on DEFENSE and overall fit (and opportunity cost).
That risk is measured in Lever 3 (defense), not here. So the offensive-collision lever does NOT
distinguish the winners from the losers; the lever that does is the defensive one.

## Placing Edwards-LaMelo (honestly, not with the winners)

- By USAGE PROFILE, Edwards-LaMelo (0.30 / 0.32) are the MOST duplicative pairing in the set:
  co-equal high usage, with LaMelo's actually higher. That profile most resembles the FAILED
  duos (Young-Murray, Booker-Beal: two co-equal ball-dominant guards), NOT the
  one-clear-primary successes (Doncic at 0.35 over Irving at 0.27).
- BUT the data says that duplication did not tank those teams' OFFENSES. So on the offensive
  collision specifically, Edwards-LaMelo's most-duplicative profile is NOT an offensive death
  sentence: the comps say the offense survives two high-usage initiators (~115-120).
- I do NOT place them with the winners. The honest placement: their offense is likely fine
  (like everyone's), they carry the most-duplicative usage profile (resembling the failures'
  shape), and whether the pairing actually fails is decided OFF the ball, by defense (Lever 3)
  and availability (Lever 5), not by an offensive collision.

## Sample-size caveat

Synergy is a difference of two noisy quantities (2,300-5,200 possessions per duo); the reliable
signal is "all small, none a large collision," NOT the rank order among them. Do not
over-interpret Fox-DeRozan -3.8 vs Morant-Bane +2.8.

## Early signal

The Edwards+LaMelo two-man offensive rating in the first 20-25 games. Expectation from the comps:
a fine offense (~115-120) even with the duplicative usage. A two-man offense well BELOW ~113
would be the surprise that says this pairing is the rare genuine offensive collision.

## Identifiability verdict

CALLABLE, and it reframes the debate: the offensive usage collision is a small effect (the bull
case's offensive worry is largely assuaged, and the bear case's "the offense collides" is not
supported), but it is NOT a load-bearing lever, because it does not separate the comparable
successes from the failures. What separated them was defense and fit. So Lever 2 neither redeems
nor dooms the trade; it redirects the decisive question to Levers 3 and 5.
