# Fit decomposition: synthesis. Built 2026-06-26.

Five levers, ranked by SWING MAGNITUDE (how much each lever's measured range moves the on-court
outcome), as pre-registered. NOT ranked by which direction they point, and NEVER collapsed into a
combined probability or a "P(fit works)" number. Can't-tell stays can't-tell; real positives are
stated as positives.

### 1. Defense (Lever 3): the largest potential swing, and genuinely unknown
Defense decides playoff series, so the lever with the most outcome-swing is LaMelo's playoff
defensive exposure. The data measured the regular-season pieces (he is a bottom-third PnR defender,
increasingly hunted; Gobert mitigates ~14-15pp at the rim), but the DECISIVE quantity, what happens
when a playoff offense pulls Gobert out and attacks LaMelo, is CAN'T-TELL: his playoff defensive
sample is essentially nil. This stays UNKNOWN, not negative. It is the highest-swing lever precisely
because its magnitude could be large in either direction and the data cannot say which.

### 2. Availability (Lever 5): the largest CALLABLE swing, leaning negative
The games-played distribution centers ~50 (median 49) against the committed 63, bimodal (28% healthy
70+, 53% compromised <=50). Swing on value: the gap between "LaMelo on" (~+1 net) and "absent,
replacement guard" (~-1.5) across a 20-game availability range is a meaningful seasonal swing, plus a
real sub-40 catastrophic tail. ROUTED THROUGH TO VALUE, not a trade-killer: a star across 50 games
still contributes real value; this is a value-REDUCTION and a tail risk, not "the trade fails." But
it is the largest callable swing and it leans the wrong way.

### 3. The offensive unlock (Lever 1 as prerequisite): the largest UPSIDE swing, conditional
This is the bull case's real path, and Lever 1 cleared its prerequisite: Edwards is an ELITE off-ball
scorer (catch-and-shoot eFG 0.63, top-four among 31 high-usage stars; off-ball is his better mode).
That is a genuine positive, stated as one: the most common offensive knock on the trade is refuted.
The SWING lives in whether the unlock then REALIZES (Edwards actually ceding on-ball reps + LaMelo
creating), which the main project's scenario layer valued at +3.5 to +7.7pp reach-CF if it clicks.
Large upside, but conditional (necessary, not sufficient) and contingent on the same availability and
defense that lean the other way.

### 4. Frontcourt fragility (Lever 4): moderate, callable, leaning negative
The trade's marginal contribution (downgrading the backup-5 from Reid to Beringer/minimums, Gueye
waived) widens the Gobert cliff and adds ~+0.8 to +1.1 net per 100 of season defensive drag vs
keeping Reid; the ABSOLUTE non-Gobert drag is larger (~2-4 per 100) and structural even at full Gobert
health. Real and negative, but a smaller marginal swing than availability or the unlock, and confirmed
non-illusory by Lever 3's box-independent rim evidence (priced once across L3 and L4).

### 5. Usage collision (Lever 2): the smallest swing, neutral
The most-debated lever moves the outcome the LEAST. Every comparable high-usage duo, successes and
failures alike, ran a fine offense with both on; the offensive collision is a small (-3.8 to +2.8),
non-separating effect. A real positive (the "they can't share the ball" fear is retired) but it does
not swing the outcome, and it pointed the decisive weight back at the defense.

## The honest summary (the line that must not tip into overstatement)

The measured UPSIDE is real but does not settle it: the offense works (L2) and Edwards can genuinely
play off-ball (L1), both credited as real positives. The largest-swing levers, however, lean the
wrong way or are unknown: availability is callable and leans negative, frontcourt depth is callable
and leans negative, and the highest-swing lever of all, playoff defense, is genuinely can't-tell. So
the lean is skeptical, but this is NOT "the trade is bad": it is "the real upside is the kind that
does not decide the outcome, while the swing sits on risks that lean negative or unknown." A star
across 50 fit-uncertain games can still be worth it; the decomposition explains WHY the merit is hard
to call, it does not declare a loss.
