## The cross-component finding

This is the most important thing this component produced.

The Wolves' two-component profile after Components 1 and 2:

- Component 1 (Ball Stickiness): 30th percentile (moderate-low)
- Component 2 (Movement Death): 71st percentile (high pickup)

The team is NOT especially sticky around a single handler, but the four off-ball players are not moving. That is a specific kind of pickup ball: scattered iso attempts by multiple players while the rest stand and watch.

The user named the four quadrants of the (stickiness, motion-death) plane:

| Quadrant | Description | Archetype |
|---|---|---|
| Q1: low sticky, low motion-death | Designed offense with ball and body movement | Warriors (gold standard) |
| Q2: high sticky, low motion-death | One creator dominates ball, but everyone moves | Brunson Knicks |
| Q3: high sticky, high motion-death | Star isolates while everyone watches | Harden Rockets |
| Q4: low sticky, high motion-death | Multiple players take iso turns while everyone watches | Wolves |

Q4 is the most damning quadrant. Q3 at least has the logic of "give it to the best player." Q4 is what happens when you have multiple players who all need touches and none of them bend the defense enough to require the others to move. It is the worst of all worlds: no offensive engine and no off-ball architecture.

This reframes the original thesis. The original thesis was "Wolves are sticky like Harden Rockets." The data says no, they're not sticky. The sharper finding: they're a **distributed-iso team without movement**, which is its own pathology.
