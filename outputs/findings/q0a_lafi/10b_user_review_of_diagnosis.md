# User review: the Wolves diagnosis memo

**Date:** 2026-05-17
**Author:** Bobby
**Context:** Read of the diagnosis memo. Phase 6 (polish) kicks off after this.

## Overall assessment

This memo would land for a serious basketball decision-maker. The structure is right, the evidence chain is clean, the framing of the central finding (novel failure mode, not a known one) is the right framing, and the limitations section is the kind of honest disclosure that makes the rest of the document credible.

If sent to a front office contact tomorrow, they would read past the first page. That is a meaningful bar.

## What's working

**Executive summary earns its claims.** "Structurally novel way that the rest of the NBA has not had to defeat at scale" is the right thesis and is supported by the rest of the document.

**Section 3 (C1 contrast) does more work than its position suggests.** The historical table of C1-extreme failure cases is the kind of concrete evidence that makes a methodological finding actionable. Reading "POR 2017-18 swept R1 at C1=98, ATL 2021-22 lost R1 at C1=98, HOU 2018-19 lost WCF at C1=100" is what convinces a reader the C1 pattern is real. Pattern recognition there is exactly what front office readers respond to.

**Section 7 (Spurs case study) lands the punch.** The 72-percentile-point gap on shot quality between MIN (83) and SAS (11) is the kind of single number that fans, analysts, and front offices all understand. Pairing it with the Wembanyama mechanism gives the basketball explanation, not just the statistical one. "This is not bad luck or a hot opponent" is declarative without being absolutist.

**Three independent fixable paths in Section 6** is the right structure for diagnosis-to-prescription handoff.

## What needs refinement before Phase 6

**1. The C4 finding is buried.** The Wolves are 45th percentile on action poverty (they have a playbook, they're choosing iso anyway). This is the most actionable single finding for the coaching staff because it implies the actions exist and are not being run. Add a paragraph or short section called "the allocation problem" naming this directly: the Wolves have a normal-breadth playbook and run iso 9.6% of the time when they should be running pick-and-roll 16% of the time. This is the only finding the head coach can act on directly without acquiring new personnel.

**2. The Edwards-tier framing in Section 6 needs more care.** "Not yet at the Luka or SGA level of solo defense-breaking" is accurate but understates what Edwards is. He is a top-15 player who scored 40 in a playoff game last year. Add a sentence: "Edwards is among the league's top scoring guards and his trajectory suggests Category A protection is achievable, but he is not yet at the tier where he can carry a motion-dead offense in playoff settings against elite defensive schemes."

**3. The "novel failure mode" claim needs hedging.** The Cavaliers in some Mitchell-Garland years have had distributed scoring without true motion. The Mavericks in some pre-Kyrie years have had this shape. Reframe as "rare and not well-precedented" rather than "novel and unprecedented." Protects against a reader who can name a comp.

**4. Section 5 (correlation anomaly) needs sharper exposition.** Replace "the components do not connect to each other the way they connect for typical NBA teams" with: "For most NBA teams, dead off-ball motion produces normal shots because someone manufactures the shot quality. For the Wolves, dead off-ball motion produces bad shots because nobody manufactures the shot quality. The motion death is a vulnerability that other teams cover with personnel; the Wolves do not have the personnel that covers it." Same insight, harder hitting.

**5. The Front Office Takeaways section overpromises in point #2.** "If he reaches Luka or SGA tier in two seasons, the Wolves get Category A protection automatically." That implicit "if" needs to be more prominent. Edwards reaching that tier is plausible but not guaranteed. Rephrase: Plan A only if his trajectory continues; Plans B and C (catch-and-shoot, system change) are the bets that do not require him to make the next leap.

## What's missing that should go in

**A short section on what would falsify this diagnosis.** Right now the limitations section disclaims uncertainty but doesn't say what would convince us we're wrong. Add "we would update this analysis if [specific things happened]." Examples: if the Wolves' 2026-27 LAFI profile shifted out of Q4 even without major roster changes, the in-house-fixable framing would need revising. If Q4 teams started appearing more frequently and winning at similar rates to Q1-Q3 teams, the "rare and dangerous" framing would need to weaken.

**A brief acknowledgment of what we did NOT find.** The original thesis was that the Wolves were extreme on LAFI overall, near the top of the league. The data refined this to "extreme on a subset, moderate on others." Naming this update explicitly in the executive summary ("Initial hypothesis was the Wolves were top-5 LAFI overall. The data refined this to top-3 on the playoff-relevant subset and ~13th overall") shows the analytical work was honest about where priors needed updating.

## What the memo gets right that is hard to do

The memo lets the most damning finding land without dramatization, and lets the most hopeful finding land without overpromising.

Damning: "There is no historical playbook for fixing distributed pickup ball with moderate stickiness, dead off-ball, and bad shot quality, because that combination has not been tried at scale." Stated plainly. No italics. No adjectives.

Hopeful: "The 2023-24 shape is reachable. Two seasons of post-KAT drift, not an unrecoverable structural change." Also plain. No false optimism.

This is the tone front office memos should have. Findings get presented at their natural weight. The writing does not try to do the persuading; the evidence does.

## What Phase 6 needs to do

1. **Build the visualizations first, then revise the memo around them.** A good memo and the right charts reinforce each other. Some findings (the four-act trajectory, the league quadrant map) will land twice as hard with the right visual.
2. **Address the refinements above.** C4 allocation problem section, Edwards framing, "novel" hedging, Section 5 punch-up, takeaway revision, falsifiability section, prior-update acknowledgment.
3. **Tighten Section 1.** The opening "where the Wolves rank" section is long-ish. The component table is the centerpiece. Surrounding exposition could lose 20-30% without losing meaning.
4. **Add one more visualization not in the original spec: the Wolves Quadrant Migration.** Show the Edwards-era trajectory on the (C1, C2) plane, with arrows from 2022-23 to 2025-26. Visualizes the four-act story as a journey through quadrant space, ending in Q4.

## After Phase 6

Pause before starting any other Q-numbered analysis. The Wolves diagnosis output is what the rest of the project plans need to be re-evaluated against. Some of the other specs may need adjustment given what we have actually found. Better to look at them all together with this in hand than to keep building forward.
