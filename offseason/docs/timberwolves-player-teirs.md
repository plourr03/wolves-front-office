Truly untouchable: just Edwards. He is the 24-year-old cornerstone, the whole point of the build, and he appears in zero credible trade scenarios. To your question of whether anyone else is untouchable, the honest answer is no, not in the hard sense. There is a protected tier they would only break up for a real star, but no second name is genuinely off the table.
The protected core (moved only for a star):

Jaden McDaniels is the swing chip. His trade rumors are heating up, he is described as clearly the Wolves' best trade chip, and the read is that they would prefer not to lose him but may have no better option if they are serious about getting Edwards help. So: kept by default, the first thing out the door if they swing big. Moderate likelihood, fully conditional on landing a star. NBAWikipedia
Naz Reid is grouped right next to McDaniels as a guy they should not move because it could mean a step back rather than forward. A sweetener only, and a reluctant one. Lower likelihood. Wikipedia
Beringer and Shannon are the young includes that show up in star packages (Beringer in Ja Morant frameworks, for example), though Finch has floated Shannon as a possible starting point guard, so they would rather develop them. Moderate as throw-ins, low as standalone moves. Basketball-Reference.com

The outgoing-salary question (this is where the real movement is):

Julius Randle is the most likely to actually move. He is the primary salary-matching piece and shows up as the outgoing money in nearly every framework. SI frames Randle and Gobert as prime trade candidates if Minnesota wants cap flexibility or a blockbuster. High likelihood, but as you said, it depends on finding a taker at a fair price rather than a pure dump. He also carries a 2027-28 player option. Heavy Sports
Rudy Gobert is the genuine toss-up, and your instinct is right. There is a real market (Hawks, Hornets, and Raptors have all been floated as suitors with strong return packages), and his trade value may never be higher. But the louder camp says don't deal him unless it is for a transcendent player like Giannis, because Minnesota's defense falls off a cliff without him. Moving him likely means embracing a gap year in 2026-27. Two mechanics that matter for any deal: he is owed $36.5M next year with a $38M player option for 2027-28 and a 7.5% trade kicker, and that kicker inflates his outgoing salary for matching. Medium likelihood, and it is almost entirely a function of whether a star becomes available. Minnesota Timberwolves + 4
Donte DiVincenzo is movable salary more than a coveted asset right now. He will be sidelined for much of 2026-27 with the Achilles, which caps his standalone value, but his contract keeps appearing as matching filler in the bigger frameworks (the Kyrie and Durant ideas both route him out). Minnesota Timberwolves

Effectively departing: Mike Conley, who the team is reportedly looking to replace at ball-handler, so plan on him aging out rather than being a trade asset. Depth pieces Bones Hyland and Jaylen Clark are worth keeping but may get squeezed if Minnesota needs the cap room. Minnesota Timberwolves
Not a trade-out, but central: Dosunmu is the top re-sign priority and the DiVincenzo replacement, so he is a keep, not a chip.
