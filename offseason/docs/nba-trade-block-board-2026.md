# NBA Trade Block Board, 2026 Offseason

**As of:** June 9, 2026 (two weeks before the June 23 draft, three before free agency). A living document, meant to be refreshed as the dominoes fall.

**A standing caveat.** Almost nothing here is a done deal. Most situations are a mix of credible reporting and analyst projection, and there is a lot of fan trade-machine noise to filter out. Every named player carries a tag so you know which is which.

## How to read the tags

Each name reads as **[Move Likelihood · Driver · Confidence]**.

- **Move Likelihood** (does this player actually change teams this summer): Very High, High, Moderate, Low, Minimal.
- **Driver** (what is pushing it): Player-driven (wants out), Team-shopping, Mutual, Cap-forced, Timeline pivot, Speculative.
- **Confidence** (sourcing quality): Reported (insider sourcing), Analyst (credible columnist or podcast read), Speculative (fan or trade-machine).

A note on the league state: there has been heavy movement since the February deadline, so a few current rosters below are my best reconstruction from reporting. Where I am unsure of a team detail I flag it.

## Tier 1: Stars and near-stars genuinely in play

**Milwaukee Bucks: Giannis Antetokounmpo [High · Mutual · Reported].** The headliner, and the one that matters most for your purposes. Milwaukee went 32-50 and missed the playoffs, snapping a nine-year streak, with reported locker-room issues and a front office rift. ESPN's Shams Charania reported back in January that Giannis had essentially indicated he is ready to move on, and that Milwaukee is listening to inquiries. As of June 7 to 8, the league-wide chatter is that **Miami is closing in and is the clear frontrunner** (HP Basketball, Chris Haynes, Matt Moore, Sam Amick), though the sourcing is "from around the league" rather than from either team. Two things make him a long shot for Minnesota specifically: Chris Haynes notes Giannis holds enormous power to steer his destination and can warn off a team he does not want by signaling he will leave after one year, and his stated concern is whether the acquiring team has enough talent left after paying the price. Milwaukee also lacks first-round picks, so the asking price is pick-heavy, which is exactly where the Wolves are thin. Bill Simmons floated a deal landing between Finals Games 4 and 5. Deadline footnote that matters for the Wolves specifically [Reported, CBS, Feb 6]: Minnesota was among the favorites for Giannis at the February deadline before Milwaukee shut down talks, which tells you the front office's appetite is real and the obstacle is Milwaukee's posture plus the pick-heavy ask. Read: most likely star to move, probably to Miami, and hard for Minnesota to win even if it gutted the roster.

**Washington Wizards: Anthony Davis [Moderate to High · Timeline pivot · Reported-adjacent].** This is your most realistic star target on salary. Washington acquired Davis from Dallas in February (sending out Khris Middleton's expiring deal), then won the No. 1 pick in a loaded draft (Dybantsa, Peterson, Boozer at the top). That changes everything: ESPN's framing is that Davis "might stick around, might be flipped," and the worry is Washington pulled its rebuild out of the oven too early. With a franchise prospect arriving, a pivot to full youth is live. Davis is owed $58.4M in 2026-27 with a $62.7M player option for 2027-28, which matters because that figure matches cleanly against a Gobert-plus-Randle package. His role and long-term commitment on a young team are the open question.

**Washington Wizards: Trae Young [Moderate · Team-shopping (soft) · Analyst].** Same timeline tension as Davis, but Washington seems more committed to keeping and extending him to pair with the rookie (a reported three-year, $156.9M extension was on the table through June 30). He carries a roughly $49M player option for 2026-27. Available in theory if they go all youth, less likely than Davis to actually move.

**Memphis Grizzlies: Ja Morant [High · Mutual · Reported].** Memphis signaled a rebuild by trading Jaren Jackson Jr. to Utah, and Morant has been smothered in trade talk since before the deadline. He fits a glaring need for you (lead guard next to Edwards) and the Edwards-Morant backcourt is the dream-scenario pairing analysts keep drawing up. The cost is the availability and off-court history that depress his market. Part of the broader star market that has been waiting on the Giannis situation to resolve.

**New Orleans Pelicans: Zion Williamson [Moderate · Mutual · Reported].** The front office is reportedly listening after another year of mixed availability and a poor Williamson-Queen frontcourt fit (a minus-11 net rating together, per Cleaning the Glass). Zion has said he did not see himself in New Orleans long-term. Enormous talent, but the injury and conditioning questions make him a high-risk, high-reward gamble, and his market reflects that.

**Dallas Mavericks: Kyrie Irving [Moderate · Timeline pivot · Analyst].** Dallas overhauled its front office, parted with Jason Kidd, and is fully pivoting to a young core around Cooper Flagg, which makes Kyrie a logical trade candidate. The caveats are real: he is working back from injury and his contract and age narrow the market. Dallas is also shopping other veterans for youth and picks (Gafford to the Lakers has been floated).

## Tier 2: High-value veterans on sellers and retoolers

**Utah Jazz: Lauri Markkanen [Moderate · Team-shopping · Analyst].** A perennial trade name, 28 and in his prime at 26.7 points a game, on a Jazz team whose direction is muddled (they bought low on JJJ but are not clearly contending). Detroit and others covet him. Available for the right young-talent-plus-picks package.

**Brooklyn Nets: Michael Porter Jr. [Moderate to High · Team-shopping · Analyst].** Classic rebuilding-team expensive vet ($40.8M), with championship pedigree from Denver. Brooklyn is stockpiling and would move him for depth and picks. Nets also have Nic Claxton as a movable veteran center.

**Sacramento Kings: DeMar DeRozan [Moderate · Cap-forced · Reported-adjacent].** Sacramento projects over the second apron, has a bloated payroll and a poor record, and wants younger talent around Keegan Murray. DeRozan, 36, is still an efficient scorer and only partially guaranteed in 2026-27, which makes him movable. De'Andre Hunter is another piece they could flip.

**Chicago Bulls: Collin Sexton / Anfernee Simons / Jaden Ivey [Moderate · Team-shopping · Analyst].** Chicago has guard surplus and these three are sign-and-trade or straight trade candidates. Nikola Vucevic hits free agency this summer. The Bulls also hold a high lottery pick, so they are building youth and can move veterans.

**LA Clippers: Bradley Beal [Low · n/a · Reported].** Worth listing only to set expectations: Beal, dealing with a hip injury, is expected to opt into his final year with the Clippers, so do not count on him moving. The Clippers reshaped around Kawhi by acquiring Darius Garland (in the Harden-to-Cleveland deal).

**LA Clippers: Kawhi Leonard [Low to Moderate · Speculative · Speculative].** Surfaces in Wolves-target lists, but I have seen no hard reporting that the Clippers are shopping him. Treat as a fan-driven idea until a credible source says otherwise. His age, injury history, and $50.3M salary are the backdrop.

## Tier 3: Contenders and buyers (hunting upgrades, generally not supplying stars)

**Oklahoma City Thunder [Buyer with surplus].** The champions face a roster-crunch decision: if they keep both 2026 first-rounders they will need to move two rotation players. So depth pieces are available, and they have an extra first to dangle. Not selling anyone of consequence.

**Miami Heat [Buyer, all-in].** The Giannis frontrunner. The pieces in play to Milwaukee would be some combination of Tyler Herro, Andrew Wiggins, Kel'el Ware, and a pile of picks and swaps. Outside a Giannis deal, they are hunting, not selling.

**New York Knicks [Buyer, mostly standing pat].** Brunson, Bridges, Hart, and Karl-Anthony Towns. KAT gets floated in hypotheticals, but there is no real indication he is available. If they had flamed out early they might rethink; absent that, expect tweaks.

**Boston Celtics [Retooling on cost, but core not for sale].** Tatum missed most of the season (Achilles), and Boston still earned the 2-seed behind Jaylen Brown. They already shed Jrue Holiday and Kristaps Porzingis for cost. Crucially, a RealGM insider says he has not heard "one iota" of Jaylen Brown or Derrick White being genuinely available, so treat Brown-to-anywhere as speculation, not reporting. [Brown: Low · Speculative · Reported-against]

**Houston Rockets [Buyer].** Added Kevin Durant last summer (for a package headlined by the No. 10 pick, Jalen Green, and Dillon Brooks). Young core plus KD; a contender, not a seller.

**Cleveland Cavaliers [Buyer].** Added James Harden for Darius Garland. Mitchell, Mobley, Allen, Harden core. Not selling, though Jarrett Allen occasionally surfaces in fit-driven hypotheticals.

**Denver Nuggets [Buyer, thin].** Jokic is untouchable. They moved Michael Porter Jr. to Brooklyn for Cam Johnson and need cheap depth, with limited picks to deal. A role player could move at the margins.

**Golden State Warriors [Buyer, aging].** Curry and Jimmy Butler core, win-now. Lost Jonathan Kuminga to Atlanta. Hunting cheap help, not selling.

**Phoenix Suns [Retooling around Booker].** Dealt Durant for Jalen Green, Dillon Brooks, and the No. 10 pick. Booker is the cornerstone. Their movable salary largely left with the Beal situation; expect them to add youth, not subtract a star.

**Orlando Magic [Buyer].** Banchero, Wagner, Suggs core, with Anthony Black ascending. They need shooting and a reserve playmaker. Limited tradeable firsts after the Bane trade (control of their own pick is murky until 2031), which caps how aggressive they can be.

**Detroit Pistons [Buyer on the rise].** Cade Cunningham core, a genuine turnaround team that needs more scoring and shooting. Targeting Markkanen and Porter Jr. types. Buyers, with only the No. 21 pick and no second-rounder as draft ammo.

**Charlotte Hornets [Young, opportunistic].** LaMelo Ball, Brandon Miller, Kon Knueppel core, wanting defensive wings. Floated as a Gobert suitor (Miles Bridges and Ryan Kalkbrenner as matching salary plus picks). Could be a real player for veterans.

**Portland Trail Blazers [Logjammed, listening].** Added Jrue Holiday, with Lillard reportedly returning, plus Scoot Henderson, Shaedon Sharpe, and Deni Avdija. There could be calls on Holiday this summer given the backcourt crunch. [Holiday: Moderate · Team-shopping · Analyst]

**San Antonio Spurs [Loaded, selectively flexible].** Wembanyama, De'Aaron Fox, Devin Vassell, plus the young guard pair of Stephon Castle and Dylan Harper. With Fox and Vassell in place, one of Castle or Harper could be viewed as semi-expendable in the right blockbuster. The team that just bounced you; not desperate, but has real surplus young talent.

## The three things this board tells you

1. **Giannis is probably not realistic for Minnesota.** He is likely Miami-bound, he controls his destination, and he wants a roster that can win immediately, which a gutted post-trade Wolves team would not be. Keep him on the board, but weight the feasibility verdict toward long shot.
2. **Anthony Davis is the most realistic star fit, on money alone.** His $58.4M lines up against a Gobert-plus-Randle package, and Washington has a real reason to consider flipping him after winning the No. 1 pick. He is the name to model first.
3. **The realistic upgrade tier is in Tier 2.** Markkanen, Porter Jr., a Bulls guard, DeRozan, Quickley, and the Memphis and Dallas vets are where gettable, need-addressing players actually live, especially given your thin pick capital. That tier is also where a Naz Reid or young-piece sweetener does the most work.
