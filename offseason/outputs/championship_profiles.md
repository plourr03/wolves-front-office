## THE ADVERSE-SELECTION SCREEN (why the pair board changed)

The first pair pass surfaced the highest-SURPLUS players (big net at low salary). That is
exactly backwards: high surplus value is the signal a team will NOT sell at par. Avdija (a
+1.9-net All-Star on a below-market $13.1M) and Camara (a high-surplus 3-and-D piece) are
cornerstones, not trade targets, the Wembanyama filter artifact at roster scale. The corrected
surfacing screens on surplus (`surplus = net - par(salary)`) and tags availability with
block-board discipline (Reported / Analyst / Speculative / KEEP), surfacing only players a team
has a real reason to move: logjams (Chicago's guards), expirings (Jalen Smith), and cost-cutters
(Phoenix's second-apron vets). Avdija + Camara remains in this doc ONLY as the labeled
durable-pair ceiling (+2.41 if it were somehow available), never on the live board.

## THE DISAGGREGATION THESIS: confirmed, but it does not dominate

Trading one $33M forward for two durable role players diversifies risk: G weights four roster
states, and **P(both hurt) is just 2-7%** for two durable players, versus the 50-68% bad-roster
probability that sank the single stars. So the risk-adjusted EV stays close to raw (gap -0.6 to
-1.7, vs the stars' -2.7+). The shallow-downside thesis HOLDS. But with realistic (not artifact)
names, the raw upside is modest, so the realistic pairs land +0.9 to +1.8, competitive with, not
dominant over, the single-player bar. The structural advantages are real and worth pricing:

- **The under-line pairs keep the WHOLE rotation.** Inbound <= $33.3M means no take-back-more
  hard cap, so Tre Jones + Smith keeps Gobert AND Dosunmu AND Conley (loses only Randle). That is
  the advantage Markkanen and Kyrie lack: Markkanen-B and Kyrie-B both must shed Dosunmu to the
  hard cap. Tre Jones + Smith is the safest floor-raiser on the board for that reason.
- **The over-line CHI Giddey pair (+1.84)** has the highest realistic raw (+3.5) but trips the
  hard cap (A/B) and asks Chicago to move its starting PG (Speculative).

## STRATEGIC SYNTHESIS (corrected)

1. **There is no dominant move, only a cluster of competitive, health-robust ones at +1.3 to +2.0.**
   The earlier "disaggregation dominates / Avdija + Camara is the best move" was an acquisition
   artifact. With realistic names the picture is a genuine choice, not a slam dunk.
2. **Higher ceiling, but a roster cost:** Markkanen-B (+2.00) and CHI Giddey + Smith (+1.84) are
   the top realistic numbers, but both trip the hard cap, Markkanen-B loses Dosunmu and the Giddey
   pair asks Chicago to move its starter and is only Speculative to acquire.
3. **Lower ceiling, no cost:** CHI Tre Jones + Jalen Smith (+1.30) keeps the entire rotation, is
   the most plainly acquirable (logjam + expiring), and is the safest floor-raiser.
4. **The fragile-star path stays closed:** AD, Morant, Trae are negative risk-adjusted; Kyrie is a
   medical-contingent option, not a plan.
5. **Giannis (+0.94) is the upside swing you bid on, not the plan** (unacquirable at par).

**The recommendation:** pursue the durable, plainly-acquirable disaggregation (Tre Jones + Smith
profile) as the safe floor-raiser that keeps the core intact, or Markkanen-B if you accept losing
Dosunmu for a higher ceiling; hold the 2027 option open rather than gambling on a fragile star;
and do not chase Giannis at a premium you cannot pay. The honest read is a competitive cluster of
mid-tier, health-robust moves, none a clear winner, with the asset cost still to be priced.

## CAPSTONE: THE OFFSEASON PORTFOLIO (range-anchored)

Single moves understate what an offseason can do, because the cheap TPE fliers (Mitchell $3.0M,
McBride $4.3M) fit inside the TPEs and STACK with a Randle conversion rather than competing.
Each portfolio is a sequence (Randle conversion + TPE flier + MLE shooter + Dosunmu Bird re-sign
+ No.28 pick), gate-validated leg-by-leg (TPE and MLE both trip the first-apron hard cap, so
order and headroom matter), with the complete roster run through F/G on the unified spine. Every
number is published as a RANGE across the four views, with DARKO as the conservative anchor:

| Portfolio | view range (box .. DARKO .. consensus .. rapm) | **published range** | gauntlet (OKC/SAS) | 2027 flex |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A: Spacing+Depth, keep core** | +0.25 / +1.75 / +3.37 / +4.20 | **+1.7 to +3.4** | +9.9 / +13.6 | HIGH |
| **C: Mitchell upside** | +0.52 / +1.62 / +3.33 / +3.50 | **+1.6 to +3.3** | +9.6 / +13.3 | HIGH |
| B: Markkanen ceiling (lose Dosunmu) | +0.75 / +0.78 / +4.32 / +5.78 | **+0.8 to +4.3** (wide) | +12.0 / +16.4 | LOW |
| D: Patience, no Randle (taxpayer-MLE best) | -0.15 / -0.57 / -0.52 / -0.50 | **~ -0.5** (priced fallback) | -1.0 / -1.8 | MODERATE |

**Range, not point (winner's-curse on our own consensus):** A's box read is +0.25 because box
metrics are blind to spacing and role value (the same blind spot that under-rates Gobert and
Markkanen); it is the explained low outlier, not the anchor. DARKO (+1.75) is the conservative
anchor, rapm (+4.20) the upside, so A publishes as +1.7 to +3.4. The decisive comparison is on
the conservative anchor: **A and C anchor at +1.6-1.75 (DARKO-corroborated); B anchors at +0.78**
(DARKO doubts the Markkanen-heavy build), so on the floor that matters A/C beat B even though B's
consensus ceiling is higher. B's edge lives only in the metrics DARKO distrusts.

**Portfolio D isolates the Randle conversion's marginal value, and patience is a priced fallback
(not free).** CBA precision: keeping Randle and re-signing Dosunmu lands ~$210.5M, over the FIRST
apron, so the full MLE and the TPE absorptions are gone, but the team is NOT frozen, it is down to
the taxpayer mini-MLE (~$6.1M, which hard-caps at the SECOND apron $222M, validated by the gate)
plus minimums. Its honest best version (mini-MLE Shamet/Kennard-tier shooter + Dosunmu, keep
Randle) prices at **~ -0.5pp of title with -1.0/-1.8pp of gauntlet erosion vs OKC and SAS** (the
young gates rise while MIN stands pat). So the Randle conversion is worth roughly the whole
+1.7-to-3.4 swing plus the avoided erosion: it is what unlocks the full tool stack. D remains the
honest floor if no counterparty materializes, not a collapse (the youth limits decay), but a real
~half-point premium paid for patience. Tax footnote (a cost the model does not price): D's
~$216.6M sits deep into the luxury tax, likely the repeater rate, to buy a roughly -0.5pp
outcome, an ownership-willingness burden that the title math ignores but a real decision would not.

**MLE shooter, named (not generic):** the modeled +0.8-net 3-and-D was conservative; real 2026
MLE-tier free agents rate higher, Norman Powell (consensus +2.09 / DARKO +1 / 76% avail), C.J.
McCollum (+1.20 / +1 / 83%), Kevin Huerter (+1.53 / +0 / 83%), so the portfolio EVs are a slight
floor. **Portfolio C side benefit:** its flier (Ajay Mitchell) comes from OKC, so it marginally
weakens the primary rival while strengthening MIN.

### The asset bill (comp-anchored lens over A, C, D)

The title board does not price the picks; laid over the top:
- **Randle to Chicago is not free.** CHI absorbs +$24M in salary ($33.3M Randle for $9.4M Smith)
  on a player DARKO grades at -$11.3M surplus, so MIN ATTACHES the sweetener. **Target structure:
  two-to-three SECONDS; a lightly-protected first is the ceiling concession, not the goal**, because
  the 2027 option value rests on keeping the first-round stash intact, so the negotiating preference
  is explicitly to pay in seconds and protect the firsts.
- **Each TPE absorption** (McBride / Mitchell) costs roughly a second.
- **Pick 28 is used XOR traded, not both.** If it is the Randle sweetener, A/C do not also draft a
  rookie with it; if it drafts the rookie, the Randle sweetener is seconds instead.
- **True 2027 flexibility after the bill:** A/C still clear Randle's $33M for a clean 2027 cap
  sheet, but the pick stash thins (a first or several seconds out). B keeps more picks but clogs
  2027 with Markkanen's ~$46M and loses Dosunmu. D keeps all picks but keeps Randle (a 2027 player
  option that clogs the sheet if exercised) and freezes the tools.
- **Verdict stability:** the asset bill does NOT flip the ranking. A/C cost ~a first plus a few
  seconds for a DARKO-anchored +1.6-1.75 floor and a clean 2027 cap; that is a proportionate price.
  No portfolio's verdict changes once the bill is paid.

### The recommendation

**Portfolio A (reliable) or C (upside): the Smith-first stacked build.** Convert Randle into the
DARKO-corroborated stretch-five, stack a TPE flier (McBride the reliable contract-year rental, or
Mitchell the corroborated upside guard, with the bonus of weakening OKC), sign an MLE 3-and-D
shooter (Powell / McCollum / Huerter tier), re-sign Dosunmu via Bird, and use or trade the No.28
pick. It lands under the first apron, anchors at +1.6-1.75 on the conservative DARKO view with a
+3.3-3.4 consensus ceiling, improves the gauntlet against OKC and SAS, and leaves 2027 clean.
**A/C also carry an internal development plan, not just a hold:** make Beringer the designated
backup-five behind an aging Gobert (the groomed-backup cone, ~46% rotation-rim+ by 2027-28) and
add NO veteran big who blocks him, and hand Shannon the bench-creator minutes that Randle's exit
and DiVincenzo's absence open (his runway cone, ~63% dependable-or-better). The cones confirm the
kids develop inside the recommendation without paying the both-out path's present-title cost.
**The Piece 2 to Piece 7 bridge (delivery system):** the Gobert usage audit
(`gobert_usage_audit.md`) found Minnesota fielded just ONE meaningful live-dribble pick-and-roll
feeder around him in 2025-26 (Edwards), down from four to six in Utah. Projecting each addition's
PnR ball-handler volume onto a full season, **A/C move that feeder count from 1 to 3** (Edwards +
Dosunmu re-signed ~208 poss + Mitchell ~357 in C; A reaches 3 only if the MLE is a PnR guard like
McCollum ~472 or Powell ~264, not a spot-up shooter like Huerter, since McBride ~133 does not clear
the bar). So the same conversion that raises the floor also rebuilds the delivery environment that
makes Gobert a weapon, restoring it toward Utah levels WITHOUT trading him, though realizing the
roll-man volume also requires running more pick-and-roll than Minnesota's below-average 2025-26 rate.
It
beats the fragile-star path (negative risk-adjusted), the higher-ceiling-but-DARKO-skeptical and
inflexible Markkanen build (B), and the apron-choked patience fallback (D). If no counterparty
materializes, D is the honest floor: run it back, the youth limits decay, and strike in 2027 when
the fragile stars' health is known.

## COMPANION: THE BOTH-OUT CONTINGENCY PLAYBOOK (Gobert reservation price)

A separate question from the A-D board: should MIN trade Gobert at all? Built with the portfolio
machinery, range-anchored. The both-out portfolios all price NEGATIVE now, because replacing a
DPOY rim anchor with a young committee (Kalkbrenner + Beringer + a vet) is the rim-protection trap:

| Portfolio | four-view (cons/box/rapm/DARKO) | now vs A | 2027 war chest |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A (keep Gobert, reference)** | +3.37 / +0.25 / +4.20 / +1.75 | -- | clean cap, firsts intact |
| B1 asset-harvest (Gobert->CHA) | -0.50 / -0.72 / -1.20 / -0.43 | **-2.2 (DARKO) to -5.4 (RAPM)** | **rich: picks + young big (Kalkbrenner) in** |
| B2 cap-space reset | (committee rim, two FA signings) | negative now | **richest: up to ~$29.9M room + picks** |
| B3 Giannis all-in | (slate long shot) | high variance | depletes picks |

**A/C win now, both-out wins later, and the gap is large now.** Trading Gobert sacrifices +2.2pp
(DARKO) to +5.4pp (RAPM) of present title equity to the rim trap, in exchange for a materially
richer 2027 war chest (B1 brings in a young rim-runner plus picks; B2 clears up to ~$29.9M of cap
room).

**The development dividend (cones), de-biased.** Because Naz is a stretch big, Beringer is the
only internal rim answer in any Gobert-out world, so his cone is load-bearing for the both-out
case. But the dividend is smaller than it first looks, for two reasons that both cut toward
holding. (1) Selection bias: minutes are endogenous, so the starter-track "runway" cone (65%
rotation-rim+ by 2027-28) is INFLATED, and the earlier "blocked = 7%" was an artifact of mixing
failed-anywhere washouts into the buried group. (2) The groomed-backup path exists WITHOUT a
trade: a young first-round big given ~700-1500 backup minutes behind a starter lands at **46%
rotation-rim+**, so keeping Gobert and grooming Beringer as the backup-five develops him nearly as
well as trading him (46% vs an inflated 65%). The trade's marginal development dividend is
therefore small and discounted, it nudges the reservation price but does not flip the RAPM-view
verdict, and the cost of KEEPING Gobert is likewise small. **Judgment flag (applies to
acquisitions too):** B1 leans on Miles Bridges, who carries a real off-court/culture flag; per the
framework's rule a red flag CAPS the realized tier regardless of the simulated number, so B1's
already-negative now read is a ceiling, not a midpoint. CBA precision on B2: dropping under the $165M cap renounces the TPEs and the MLE for cap
space plus the room exception, and Dosunmu's Bird rights survive ONLY if his cap hold is kept
(keep-hold leaves ~$13.4M of room; renounce-hold maxes ~$29.9M but Dosunmu walks), so the gate
models both regimes. Two-sided destination: at the TITLE-ODDS level, arming the Lakers costs only
~+0.02pp (LAL is not a title gate for MIN), so the rival-arming concern is a matchup/seeding point
more than a championship-probability one, smaller than intuition suggests, but it still argues for
the non-rival CHA destination, which also returns the better rim-solving young big.

### The headline: the Gobert reservation price (by view)

Keeping Gobert preserves the option to trade him later (at the deadline, or once his value clears);
trading now destroys that reverse option. The walk-away threshold, the minimum return at which
trading this summer beats running A/C and holding the option, depends entirely on WHICH Gobert
valuation you believe, because his four-view spread is the widest in the project:

| view of Gobert | now-EV sacrificed | return must clear (incl. lost option) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| box (+1.48) | +0.97pp | ~+1.5pp (~2-3 quality firsts + young big) | **MODERATE: a CHA-type harvest clears it** |
| DARKO (+2.00) | +2.18pp | ~+2.7pp (~4-5 firsts) | HIGH: rarely met |
| consensus (+5.28) | +3.87pp | ~+4.4pp (~7 firsts) | essentially unclearable |
| rapm (+5.76) | +5.40pp | ~+5.9pp (~10 firsts) | unclearable, hold |

**The reservation price is high under every view except the most Gobert-skeptical (box).** Only if
you genuinely believe the box read (+1.48, a good-not-irreplaceable center) does a realistic
CHA-type package (two-to-three firsts plus a young rim-running big like Kalkbrenner) clear the bar,
and even then only if you are optimizing 2027 over 2026-27. Under DARKO, consensus, and RAPM the
rim is too costly to give up for any realistic return. **Verdict: hold Gobert and run Portfolio
A/C; the reverse option is real at the FEBRUARY 2027 DEADLINE but largely gone by summer 2027.** The
depreciation curve (`gobert_depreciation_curve.md`) makes this precise: his contract surplus crosses
NEGATIVE this coming season (+$4.7M today to about -$1.3M in 2026-27 as the salary steps to $36.5M
and age 34 bites), and reaches about -$8M on the $38M age-35 option year, so the realistic return
falls from a real package now, to a contender's deadline premium, to paying-to-move (the Horford 2020
case, where PHI attached a first to dump the contract) by summer 2027. Holding past the deadline destroys more value than any summer-2027 return
adds. Under the box and DARKO views the depreciation SOFTENS the hold into a sell-at-the-deadline
lean; under consensus and RAPM the hold-and-contend verdict survives. The both-out playbook is the
contingency you keep on the shelf, and the deadline is the moment you re-decide.
