# Findings: the optimal-trade board (every team)

_A names-named companion to the portfolio capstone. For each of the 29 partners, the model
searched legal two-team trades and returned the single deal that maximizes Minnesota's
risk-adjusted championship dP on the aged spine, subject to the partner actually accepting it
(acceptance from data: surplus-improvement, need-fill, or a data-gated cap-relief dump). Then it
tried targeted three-team deals to unlock the near-misses. The point is not to find a blockbuster.
It is to ask the league, one team at a time, whether there is a deal that beats running back the
recommended plan, and to report honestly when the answer is no._

## The headline: these are coin-flip bets, and the plan already beats them

Read the conservative anchor, not the top line. Every surfaced deal carries a four-view range, and
on the best deals the skeptical floor (the box and DARKO views, which are bearish on role players)
sits at or below zero: Milwaukee's anchor is -0.63, Chicago's is +0.03, Sacramento's is -0.93. The
consensus and RAPM views like these additions; the box-based views say they may not move
Minnesota's title number at all. That gap is the finding. None of these deals is a sure thing.
They are zero-to-plus-two bets, with a real chance of being worth nothing, that cost one or two
first-round picks.

And once you price both axes the capstone uses, present title equity and 2027 flexibility together,
**no deal on the board beats Portfolios A and C.** The best deal the league offers (Milwaukee, at
+1.98 risk-adjusted) barely edges Portfolio A's +1.75, and it does so while turning the 2027 sheet
from clean to clogged. Chicago, the best fit, lands at +1.60, a tie with Portfolio C's +1.62, at the
same flexibility cost. There is no deal that is both better now and keeps the war chest. The trade
market sells Minnesota a marginal, win-now, flexibility-destroying upgrade, or it sells nothing.

That is the story. The rest is the evidence.

## The board, by the numbers

Of 29 partners: 13 produce a deal that is both legal and acceptable to the other side, 8 clear a
positive risk-adjusted bar, and every one of those 8 is LOW or MODERATE 2027 flexibility. Sixteen
partners return nothing. The targeted three-team pass unlocked nothing. No Gobert-out deal won
anywhere.

The board does not have a deal that clears either while keeping the war chest.

## The two corrections that changed the ranking

This board is the second pass. Two fixes moved it, and both moved it toward honesty.

- **The two-center deals were re-scored under realistic lineups.** The first pass parked every
  acquired big in Randle's vacated starter slot, which played him next to Gobert and inflated the
  consensus view. Now a non-shooting center acquired while keeping Gobert is forced to backup
  minutes, because two non-spacers cannot share the floor. The effect is exactly what the eye test
  predicts: **Dallas's Daniel Gafford, a redundant rim presence, fell from the top of the board to a
  backup throw-in, while Milwaukee's Myles Turner, a true stretch-five who spaces next to Gobert,
  held his value and rose to number one.** Turner passes Gafford once the lineup is real. That
  distinction is the whole reason the ranking is trustworthy now.

- **The Utah picks were held.** The first pass let the two incoming Utah firsts (2027 and 2029) pad
  every sweetener bill by default. They carry protection and Stepien conditions that have to be read
  per deal, so they are now held out of the live search. Every bill above is paid with Minnesota's
  own, unconditioned chips only: this year's pick, the 2028 and 2033 own firsts, and seconds. **No
  surfaced deal required a Utah first**, so no conditional-pick read was forced. The bills are real
  and they are not cheap: Milwaukee and Chicago each cost this year's pick plus the 2028 own first.

## The honest no-fit majority

Sixteen partners return nothing worth doing, and that is the adverse-selection screen working, not
a hole in the search:

- **Contenders do not sell and cannot absorb.** Oklahoma City, Boston, Miami, and Detroit have
  nothing available that Minnesota wants and cannot take back Randle's salary anyway.
- **Cornerstones are not for sale at this price.** The screen holds every young max player (Luka,
  Haliburton, Brunson, Sengun, Morant) and every cost-controlled ascending asset (Risacher, the
  young centers) off the board. No team trades a 25-year-old franchise pillar for a 31-year-old
  Randle and a pick. This is the same discipline that keeps McDaniels and Naz off Minnesota's own
  outbound list.
- **The willing sellers' best pieces are marginal or speculative.** Utah will move Markkanen and
  Memphis its veterans, but once priced and risk-adjusted, none clears the bar over running the plan.

## Speculative by construction: the star deals are not a market

Two surfaced deals, Dallas (Kyrie) and Utah (Markkanen), involve a player the posture math lets
through only because his team profiles as a pivoter. **These are Speculative by construction, not
reported availability.** Nothing in this analysis says Dallas is shopping Kyrie or that a Davis-type
market exists. The model surfaces a $38M-plus star only when his team's data looks like a teardown,
and it tags the result so the board never implies a market that has not been reported. Washington
will, in the model, move Anthony Davis, and the deal is legal, but AD's injury history (49 percent
recency-weighted availability) sinks it below the no-fit line on its own. Minnesota can get him. It
should not want him. Treat every star name here as a hypothetical the cap sheet permits, not a rumor.

## Cross-validation: keep-Gobert dominates everywhere

The cap-relief channel was built so the model could represent shedding salary purely to open 2027
flexibility, and a specific check was whether it would rediscover the both-out Gobert harvest the
playbook already priced. It did not, and the reason is informative. **No Gobert-out or both-out deal
won its partner anywhere on the board.** Gobert carries a +3.08 surplus (he is a good contract, not
a bad one), so no team treats acquiring him as a favor that needs a sweetener, and keeping him while
converting Randle simply scores higher on present title equity than any deal that ships him out.
This is the same conclusion championship_profiles reached by hand: A and C win now, the both-out
reset wins later, and the gap now is large. The blind league-wide sweep confirms it.

## Three-team: nothing unlocked

The targeted three-team pass took the near-misses (mostly contenders who wanted to deal but could
not absorb Randle's $33M) and looked for a dump-absorbing third team to route the salary. None
resolved into a deal where all three sides clear both acceptance and feasibility. The honest reading:
three-team deals unlock salary-matching problems, not willingness problems, and Minnesota's blockers
here are willingness, not matching.

## What would change these verdicts

- **A contender pivots.** If Oklahoma City, Boston, or Miami unexpectedly makes a real player
  available, the board changes. Nothing in the data predicts it.
- **Minnesota spends its crown-jewel picks AND the held Utah firsts.** The search paid with the
  cheap end of the chest first and never reached for the conditional Utah picks. Authorizing them, and
  the 2028 and 2033 own firsts together, would raise a few ceilings, at a flexibility cost the
  capstone already argues against.
- **A surfaced player's exact cap hit clears the match.** Salary matching is exact-number sensitive,
  and the contract data is Pass-1 scrape quality (the Zubac-on-Indiana error is the reminder). Every
  surfaced player's precise 2026-27 cap hit needs a Spotrac verify before any of these is called real.

## Bottom line

The trade board is a 29-team confirmation of the capstone, reached from the opposite direction. The
league offers Minnesota a few modest, win-now, flexibility-destroying bets and a large majority of
nothing, and not one of them beats running Portfolio A or C once you price 2027. If the front office
wants to act anyway, Chicago for Giddey is the deal that addresses the actual roster need, and it
should be understood as spending 2027 to buy half-court creation now, with a conservative floor near
zero, not as an upgrade the model endorses.

_Method note. Built by trade_search.py on the existing championship-impact engine (apply_trade ->
bracket_sim, four views, risk overlay) plus a new partner-side layer: an all-30-team need vector
(build_team_needs.py), a league-wide surplus and data-derived posture layer (build_value_layer.py),
and a three-channel acceptance model (partner_acceptance.py). Tiered for cost: a free
feasibility-plus-acceptance-plus-proxy screen on every candidate, then the full four-view
risk-adjusted treatment on each partner's top three survivors, with the winner chosen on the
risk-adjusted axis so a high-variance star cannot beat a steadier deal on a raw number. Realistic
lineups (no two non-spacing centers heavy minutes together). Utah incoming firsts held pending a
per-deal Stepien read. The ranked board and no-fit verdicts are in optimal_trade_board.md; the
machine-readable board is in data/trade_board.csv. Numbers are model output, not reporting;
acceptance models willingness, not a confirmed market._
