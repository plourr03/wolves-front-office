# The human look (so it does not read as AI)

Default generators reach for the same handful of moves, and stacking them is exactly what makes something look machine-made. Avoid them on purpose, and keep this look identical across the voiceover clips and the silent posts so the whole feed reads as one publication.

## The tells to avoid
Glowing neon bars on a dark background. The red, yellow, green traffic-light palette. Fully rounded lozenge bars and pill labels. One geometric bold sans doing every job. Everything dead-centered. A pile of competing reference lines and boxes.

## The choices that read human
- One accent plus a neutral, flat. Pick a single accent color for emphasis and a quiet neutral for everything else. No glow, no drop shadows, matte fills. Let intensity, not a rainbow of hues, signal good or bad. Reserve the brand green as the signature.
- Real type. A characterful headline face (a serif like Fraunces or Spectral, or a heavy condensed like Anton) paired with a monospace for the numbers (mono numerals read deliberate and line up cleanly), and a clean sans like Inter for labels. In Remotion, load real fonts with @remotion/google-fonts.
- Squared bars, not pills. Corner radius around 3 to 4, not a full lozenge.
- Restraint on chrome. One thin reference line, not three. Fewer gridlines. Direct labels over legends.
- A grid with intention. Left-aligned spine, consistent margins, a small footer credit line, an issue-number touch. Intentional asymmetry reads human, perfect symmetry reads template.
- Texture. A whisper of grain over the background, around 5 percent. This is the single fastest signal that a person made this in a design tool.
- Weighty motion. Use a slower, confident ease rather than a springy bounce, and tabular figures so counting numbers do not jiggle width as they climb.
