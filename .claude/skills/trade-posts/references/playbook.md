# Trade carousel playbook

## The data rule comes first
Every number on every slide comes from the trade model run for this specific trade. Nothing is eyeballed or estimated. Cap and apron figures especially must be exact, because people fact-check them hard, and a wrong number from an account whose whole pitch is "I have a model" is fatal. For the full never-fabricate contract and the provenance workflow, see the data-integrity reference in the social-visuals skill. Here the model is the source of truth the way the warehouse is there.

## The six slides
Hook first, receipts after. Portrait, 1080 x 1350, so it fills the phone.

1. The hook. The trade in one line plus the single most provocative number (usually the title-odds delta) or a bold verdict, and a question. This is the only slide most people see before deciding whether to swipe, so it has to stop the scroll on its own. Example: "Should the Wolves trade for Fox?" with a big "+X% title odds."
2. The deal. Who goes where, with salaries. Clean, just the pieces.
3. The cap reality. Where each team's books land against the tax line and the two aprons after the trade. This is the credibility slide, the thing almost no other account gets right. A simple salary scale with the three thresholds marked and each team placed on it.
4. The title odds. Before and after for the team that matters, as a bar or two big numbers. The model's projected championship probability.
5. Would they say yes. The model's probability each side accepts, framed as the model's estimate. This is your spiciest and most ownable slide.
6. The verdict. Your call plus a question to the room ("Wolves fans, pulling the trigger?"). Ending on the question is what pulls comments.

## Framing guardrails
- Hypothetical, not news. Put a small "hypothetical" or "trade idea" tag on the post (slide one is a good spot) so you are never mistaken for a rumor or report account.
- Modeled numbers are estimates, and you say so. "My model says Sacramento accepts 70 percent of the time." Same for the title-odds delta. That honesty is also the hook, since nobody else has those numbers.
- The recurring engine. "Drop a trade in the comments and I'll run it through the model" turns your audience into your content pipeline and maxes out comments.

## The look
Same human, editorial tokens as the rest of the feed: one accent plus a neutral, flat fills, a serif or condensed headline, monospace numerals, a whisper of grain, squared corners, restraint on chrome, a wordmark footer, and a page indicator.
