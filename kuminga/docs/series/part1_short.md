# The Bet, Part 1: What Connelly did (feed version)

Thursday, September 10, Target Center. Jonathan Kuminga, introduced on a two-year, $12.4 million deal, is asked why he's here: "Especially Ant, he called me every single time." He turned down more money and more years from the Lakers to be standing there. That's the end of the story. To see what it cost, go back to June.

The season ended June 13 on a night Minnesota wasn't playing. Dosunmu re-signed for five years and $112 million before the draft. Then the trade: Randle to Brooklyn, Naz Reid to Charlotte, the 2033 first unprotected, swap rights in 2028, 2029 and 2030, three seconds and two sets of draft rights out; LaMelo Ball, Green, Isaiah Evans and a second back. Conley, Anderson, Ingles and Phillips left in July. DiVincenzo's Achilles took most or all of the season before anyone signed anything.

Then the chase, all summer: Connelly in Miami, the general manager's 94 phone calls with the agent, Ant and LaMelo on the phone. And then Saturday, August 29, when Kuminga's $6,064,000 put Minnesota $1,999,829 over a hard cap. They traded Green and cash to Utah for Cody Williams and John Konchar, waived Konchar that afternoon, stretched him at $2,055,000 a year through 2028-29, and signed Kuminga with $4,608,584 to spare.

In dollars for this season, $75.8 million came in across seven players and $80.8 million went out across seven. The market priced it at 3.16% to win the title, sixth in the league; the grades ran from D+ to A- and argued about LaMelo. The summer got graded as a gamble. It ended with a player choosing Minnesota. Part 2 is what we found.
