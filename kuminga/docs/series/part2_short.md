# The Bet, Part 2: What we found (feed version)

Did the summer make them better or worse? Honest answer: it depends on Cody Williams's minutes. The model gives him 16.1 a night; below 12.6, the four ways we score players stop agreeing the summer hurt. And it depends on whether you count DiVincenzo's Achilles, 0.39 points of title odds on its own, as a decision or as weather. Those are the same wound: a healthy Donte is the player who takes Williams's minutes.

Three things the model is sure about. Kuminga is a real upgrade on whoever else would play the four, 0.44 points of title odds, and it held under every test; the only thing that flips it is Beringer taking the minutes, on 7.9 minutes a game of rookie evidence. LaMelo in is the one clearly positive move, 0.79 points; Naz Reid out is clearly negative, 0.33. And Anthony Edwards: of the 150 highest-volume scorers he sits at the 1st percentile for what his primary defender takes off him, and over three seasons and 245 scorers he is the lowest of all of them, with 61% of his makes unassisted against a league median of 35%. New star pairings cost shots, not efficiency. That's why LaMelo is here.

The twist: most of what we argue about in September can't be measured. Zero of eight regular-season traits predicted 195 playoff series. "Defense travels" points the other way.

The model has Minnesota at 1.68% against the market's 3.16%; the biggest disagreement is Boston, 18.33% against 5.47%. If the odds can't see what wins, what did the last eleven champions have? Part 3.
