# The Bet, Part 4: Why you should watch (feed version)

This one is my case, so start with everything that could go wrong. LaMelo's games: 51, 75, 36, 22, 47, 72. Play him 50 instead of 82 and the title odds barely move, but the chance of a top-six seed drops 16.6 points. Cody Williams's minutes are a coin flip. Kuminga can opt out, and if he's good enough to, the most Minnesota can pay him without cap room is $7,276,800. The 2033 first is gone unprotected. They're $8,062,416 over the first apron, and Konchar's $2,055,000 a year lands through Anthony Edwards's walk year.

Now the argument the grades missed. Ant is signed through 2028-29 with no options. The supermax only opens if he makes All-NBA this season, which takes 65 games, and he played 61. On a .600 team the chance he leaves is 0.8% this season, 7.3% next, and 44% at the 2028-29 cliff; on a .450 team, 56%. Stars in a walk year on .600 teams left 31% of the time; under .500, 56%. Every C+ grades the summer against a safe alternative, and the clock says it doesn't exist.

The case: LaMelo is the clear win, 0.79 points of title odds. Kuminga is a real upgrade on the alternative, 0.44, and it held under every test. Losing any one of the top three costs 54% of the odds, but it's three players now, not one. And Rudy gets a real passer for the first time, which nothing in the model can see.

The lines are drawn: Williams below 12.6 at game 20, Boston below -1.7 at game 30, Ant's true shooting below 0.547. This is a season to be in the building for.
