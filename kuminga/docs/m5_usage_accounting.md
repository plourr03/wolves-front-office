**What it shows.** Last season's usage rates do not fit on one floor. Minnesota's projected top five by minutes adds up to 1.121, above 87% of the 7,380 starting fives used in the last three regular seasons. Put Kuminga in for Dosunmu and it is 1.150 (percentile 95.6), higher than the median starting five of every one of the 90 team-seasons in that span; the highest team-season median was NOP 2023-24 at 1.146. At three-season rates, which do not let Kuminga's 36-game season set his share, that five is 1.177, percentile 97.7. So usage will be given up, and the question is what that has cost before. In 34 player-seasons where a high-usage player gained a new high-usage partner, he lost 1.9 points of usage against 0.7 for high-usage players who did not. Most of that gap is who those players were (higher prior usage, more team changes). Adjusted for it, the pairing itself cost 0.4 points of usage (SE 0.5) and 0.1 points of true shooting (SE 0.5). Among stars only (high usage at 0.28 or more, where Edwards at 0.309 and Ball at 0.306 both sit), the usage cost is larger, 1.5 points (SE 0.9), and efficiency still does not fall (+0.8, SE 1.0), though that rests on 9 treated player-seasons. The players who changed teams into a pairing, Ball's case, lost the most usage (3.2) and true shooting (1.5), but players who changed teams without a new partner lost 2.0 and 1.2, so most of that is the move. On creation, Edwards and Ball are the two self-creators: 61% and 55% of their baskets unassisted, percentiles 95 and 84. Both create far more of their twos than their threes (Edwards 70% and 44%, Ball 75% and 38%). Kuminga (44%, percentile 69, on only 157 makes) and Hyland (43%, percentile 67) are above the league median of 35% but well short of the two stars; Dosunmu (35%) is at it. **What it does not show.** Whose usage gives, because there is no lineup table and every rate here is season-level on a team, not possessions with a given partner on the floor. Whether these 34 player-seasons resemble this one: they are mostly deliberate star acquisitions, not random, and 34 treated player-seasons is a small base. The changed-teams rows rest on 15 and 11 player-seasons. None of the adjusted effects clears two standard errors at any threshold (largest 1.7). And nothing about defence, fit or winning: a pairing that costs no efficiency can still cost games. Kuminga does not meet the high-usage definition (0.226 over 831 minutes), so the base rates speak to Edwards and Ball, not to him.

*Definitions.* Usage is possession-weighted from game logs; a five's sum is the sum of its players' season rates, and a league starter's rate is his rate for that team that season. Starters carry more usage than the bench, so a starting five's sum normally runs above 1.00. Starters are read from the box score (exactly five per team-game, 7,380 team-games, 2023-24 to 2025-26). High usage: 0.25 or more over 1000+ minutes the season before. Treated: 800+ minutes for the main team and 25+ shared games with a high-usage player he did not share a game with the season before. Adjusted effect: OLS of the change on treatment, prior usage, age and team change, standard errors clustered by player. Assisted: the play-by-play description names an assister; play-by-play makes reconcile to box-score FGM. Creation percentile among the 214 players with 200+ makes.
