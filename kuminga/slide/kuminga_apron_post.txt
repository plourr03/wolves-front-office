Minnesota agreed to terms with Jonathan Kuminga this week. As of Thursday they could not sign him for the money they had agreed to pay him, and the gap is $249,829.

Here is the arithmetic. Thirteen players are under contract for 2026-27 at $215,871,829. The taxpayer mid-level exception is worth $6,064,000. Add them and you get $221,935,829. The second apron sits at $221,686,000. Using the taxpayer mid-level hard-caps a team at the second apron for the rest of the league year, and the test is where you sit after the signing, not before. So the exception is available to them and it does not fit. $249,829 is less than a rookie minimum contract.

Now the part most accounts will miss. An exception can be used partially. Minnesota has $5,814,171 of room under the apron, which is 95.9% of the mid-level, so they can sign Kuminga today for that. What they cannot do is sign him for the whole thing.

Two things close that door. It costs Kuminga $512,149 across the two years, and he is the player who just turned down more money from the Lakers to protect his own leverage. And it freezes the roster. Sitting exactly on the apron, Minnesota cannot add a fifteenth man, take back a dollar in any trade, or replace an injured player, for the entire season.

Which is why Saturday matters. August 29 is the last day to waive a player and stretch his 2026-27 salary, and Josh Green's $14,679,012 is what has to move. Trade him in a clean salary dump and they land $400,183 under the first apron at a legal 14-man roster. Stretch him instead and they sit $4.5M over it, carrying $4,893,004 of dead money in each of the next two seasons, the second of which lands in Anthony Edwards's walk year.

Cap figures verified against contracts as of August 26. Kuminga's terms are reported, not yet official.
